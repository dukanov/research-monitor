📄 **Do Audio LLMs Listen or Read? Analyzing and Mitigating Paralinguistic Failures with VoxParadox** — Introduces VoxParadox, an adversarial benchmark that tests whether Audio LLMs genuinely understand paralinguistic cues (tone, emotion) from speech or merely rely on transcript content by creating examples where text claims contradict actual speaking style. The work reveals current models achieve only 17-37% accuracy and proposes technical solutions including Prompt-Conditioned Layer Mixer and Direct Preference Optimization that improve performance to 54-65%. This differs from existing evaluation approaches by systematically exposing the gap between claimed audio understanding and actual reliance on text. [Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27772)

📄 **Comprehensive Benchmarking of Long-Form Speech Generation in Diverse Scenarios** — Proposes SwanBench-Speech, a benchmark with 1,101 samples across 17 scenarios specifically designed to evaluate long-form speech generation using 7 automated metrics covering acoustics, semantics, and expressiveness. Unlike existing benchmarks that focus on limited domains, this work specifically targets consistency and coherence issues in long-context speech generation. Results show current models struggle significantly with highly expressive scenarios and maintaining hierarchical structure across long utterances. [Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28618)

📄 **Unlocking Fine-Grained and Within-Utterance Speaking Style Control in Prompt-Based Text-to-Speech Models** — Enables smooth style interpolation between utterances using direction vectors in embedding space and within-utterance style transitions through KV-cache swapping and sliding-window attention masking. This addresses limitations of existing prompt-based TTS systems that typically apply uniform styles across entire utterances. The approach demonstrates quantitative improvements in style control while maintaining speaker similarity and perceptual smoothness. [Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27376)

📄 **Bridging the Stability-Expressivity Gap: Synthetic Data Scaling and Preference Alignment for Low-Resource Spoken Language Models** — Addresses the trade-off in low-resource languages where synthetic data improves phonetic accuracy but reduces prosodic variability, leading to monotonous speech. Proposes self-alignment frameworks (DGSA and TDSC) that recover expressivity without requiring additional real speech data, using disentanglement of prosody and timbre or temperature-driven self-critique. This differs from conventional approaches by focusing specifically on the synthetic data limitation in low-resource settings rather than requiring more training data. [Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27383)

🤖 **Darwin-TTS-1.7B-Cross-Qwen3Tokenizer** — Demonstrates cross-modal weight blending where 3% of feed-forward network weights from a language model (Qwen3-1.7B) are directly transferred to a TTS model to add emotional expressiveness without any training. The approach relies on identical architectures between source and target models, enabling direct weight arithmetic transfer. This differs from existing emotion-enhanced TTS by avoiding the need for emotional speech datasets or training time, though it requires architecturally compatible models and is sensitive to blend ratios. [Link](https://huggingface.co/zeropointnine/Darwin-TTS-1.7B-Cross-Qwen3Tokenizer)
