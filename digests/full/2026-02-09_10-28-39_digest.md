### [STACodec: Semantic Token Assignment for Balancing Acoustic Fidelity and Semantic Information in Audio Codecs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06180)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

STACodec introduces a neural audio codec that balances acoustic fidelity with semantic understanding by integrating semantic information from self-supervised learning models into the first layer of residual vector quantization through semantic token assignment. Unlike existing hybrid codecs that suffer reconstruction quality degradation when incorporating semantic information via distillation, STACodec uses a targeted approach with a semantic pre-distillation module that can predict semantic tokens directly during inference without requiring SSL-based tokenizers. The method demonstrates improved performance on both audio reconstruction and downstream semantic tasks compared to existing hybrid approaches.

**Key points:**

- STACodec introduces semantic token assignment (STA) to integrate semantic information from self-supervised learning models directly into the first layer of residual vector quantization, addressing the trade-off between acoustic fidelity and semantic information in existing codecs
- The semantic pre-distillation (SPD) module eliminates reliance on SSL-based semantic tokenizers during inference by predicting semantic tokens directly, improving computational efficiency
- Traditional codecs preserve acoustic details well but lack semantic information, while recent hybrid codecs that attempt to incorporate semantics through distillation often degrade reconstruction performance
- Experimental results demonstrate STACodec outperforms existing hybrid codecs in both audio reconstruction quality and downstream semantic tasks, achieving superior balance between acoustic fidelity and semantic capability

### [PersonaPlex: Voice and Role Control for Full Duplex Conversational Speech Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06053)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

PersonaPlex introduces a duplex conversational speech model that can dynamically control both the conversational role (e.g., customer service agent) and voice characteristics using hybrid prompts that combine text-based role conditioning with voice cloning from speech samples. Unlike existing duplex models that are limited to fixed roles and voices, this approach enables personalized, role-driven interactions by training on synthetic conversational data generated from open-source LLMs and TTS models. The model demonstrates improved role adherence, speaker similarity, and conversational naturalness compared to current state-of-the-art systems, though it relies entirely on synthetic training data.

**Key points:**

- PersonaPlex introduces hybrid system prompts that combine role conditioning with text prompts and voice cloning with speech samples, enabling flexible role and voice control in duplex conversational speech models
- Unlike existing duplex speech models restricted to fixed roles and voices, PersonaPlex supports structured, role-driven applications and personalized interactions through dynamic conditioning
- The model is trained on large-scale synthetic datasets of paired prompts and user-agent conversations generated using open-source LLMs and TTS models, demonstrating a scalable training approach
- PersonaPlex surpasses state-of-the-art duplex speech models in role adherence, speaker similarity, latency, and naturalness across multi-role customer service scenarios
- The work extends Full-Duplex-Bench benchmark beyond single assistant roles to multi-role customer service scenarios, providing better evaluation framework for role-conditioned speech systems

### [VowelPrompt: Hearing Speech Emotions from Text via Vowel-level Prosodic Augmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06270)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

VowelPrompt combines large language models with vowel-level prosodic features (pitch, energy, duration) converted to natural language descriptions for speech emotion recognition. Unlike existing LLM approaches that only use text transcriptions, this method leverages the linguistic principle that vowels carry primary affective prosody, enabling joint reasoning over semantics and fine-grained prosodic variation. The authors use a two-stage training procedure (supervised fine-tuning + reinforcement learning) and demonstrate improved performance across multiple benchmark datasets in various evaluation settings.

**Key points:**

- Novel framework VowelPrompt augments LLMs with vowel-level prosodic cues, converting pitch, energy, and duration features from time-aligned vowel segments into natural language descriptions to enable joint reasoning over lexical semantics and fine-grained prosody
- Unlike prior LLM approaches that neglect prosodic information, VowelPrompt leverages phonetic evidence that vowels are primary carriers of affective prosody for improved emotion recognition interpretability
- Two-stage adaptation procedure combines supervised fine-tuning with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enhance reasoning and enforce structured output adherence
- Consistently outperforms state-of-the-art emotion recognition methods across zero-shot, fine-tuned, cross-domain, and cross-linguistic evaluation conditions on diverse benchmark datasets
- Enables generation of interpretable explanations jointly grounded in contextual semantics and fine-grained prosodic structure, addressing both effectiveness and interpretability limitations of existing approaches

### [AudioSAE: Towards Understanding of Audio-Processing Models with Sparse AutoEncoders](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.05027)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper applies Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to audio processing models (Whisper and HuBERT) to interpret their internal representations, showing that SAE features capture both acoustic and semantic information while remaining stable across training runs. Unlike previous work that focused on text models, this systematically evaluates SAEs across all encoder layers of audio models and demonstrates practical applications like reducing Whisper's false speech detections by 70%. The authors also find that SAE features correlate with human EEG activity during speech perception, suggesting alignment with biological audio processing.

**Key points:**

- First extensive application of Sparse Autoencoders to audio processing models (Whisper and HuBERT), demonstrating over 50% feature consistency across random seeds while preserving reconstruction quality
- SAE features effectively capture and disentangle both general acoustic/semantic information and specific events including environmental noises and paralinguistic sounds (laughter, whispering), requiring removal of only 19-27% of features to erase concepts
- Feature steering achieves practical utility by reducing Whisper's false speech detections by 70% with negligible impact on Word Error Rate, demonstrating real-world applicability
- SAE features show correlation with human EEG activity during speech perception, indicating potential alignment with human neural processing mechanisms
