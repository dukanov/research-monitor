### [Hierarchical Decoding for Discrete Speech Synthesis with Multi-Resolution Spoof Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05373)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces MSpoof-TTS, a training-free inference framework that improves discrete speech synthesis by using multi-resolution spoof detectors to identify unnatural patterns in neural codec sequences at different temporal granularities, then applying hierarchical decoding to prune low-quality candidates and re-rank hypotheses. The key innovation is that it enhances synthesis quality during inference without requiring model retraining or preference optimization, instead using discriminator-guided generation to address token-level artifacts and distributional drift. The approach appears promising but as a recent method, broader validation across different models and datasets would strengthen the claims.

**Key points:**

- Introduces MSpoof-TTS, a training-free inference framework that improves neural codec language model speech synthesis without requiring model retraining or parameter modification
- Proposes Multi-Resolution Token-based Spoof Detection that evaluates codec sequences at different temporal granularities to detect locally inconsistent or unnatural patterns
- Develops hierarchical decoding strategy with progressive pruning and re-ranking of hypotheses guided by spoof detectors to address token-level artifacts and distributional drift
- Demonstrates discriminator-guided generation approach that enhances robustness of codec-based speech synthesis while maintaining zero-shot capabilities

### [WavSLM: Single-Stream Speech Language Modeling via WavLM Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05299)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

WavSLM introduces a speech language model that quantizes self-supervised WavLM representations into a single codebook and trains with autoregressive next-chunk prediction, jointly modeling semantic and acoustic information in one token stream. Unlike existing approaches, it requires no text supervision, text pretraining, or hierarchical token streams, maintaining the simplicity of single-stream generative pretraining from text language models. The method achieves competitive performance on speech generation tasks while using fewer parameters and less training data than more complex alternatives.

**Key points:**

- WavSLM introduces a novel single-stream speech language model that extends the simple autoregressive training paradigm from text LLMs to speech by quantizing and distilling WavLM representations into a single codebook
- Unlike existing approaches that require text supervision, hierarchical token streams, or complex hybrid architectures, WavSLM jointly models semantic and acoustic information within a single token stream without any text supervision or pretraining
- The method addresses the fundamental challenge of speech language modeling where semantic and acoustic information are entangled, using autoregressive next-chunk prediction on quantized speech representations
- Despite its architectural simplicity, WavSLM achieves competitive performance on consistency benchmarks and speech generation while using fewer parameters, less training data, and enabling streaming inference capabilities

### [A Large-Scale Probing Analysis of Speaker-Specific Attributes in Self-Supervised Speech Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05310)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper conducts a large-scale probing analysis of 11 self-supervised speech models to understand how they encode speaker-specific information, systematically decomposing speaker identity into acoustic, prosodic, and paralinguistic attributes across different network layers. The key finding challenges the conventional wisdom that final layers contain purely linguistic information - larger models actually recover speaker identity in their deep layers, while intermediate layers capture dynamic prosody better than specialized speaker embeddings. The work provides systematic insights into SSL model internals at unprecedented scale, offering guidelines for selecting optimal representations for different speech tasks.

**Key points:**

- Challenges the consensus that final layers of SSL models purely abstract linguistic content - larger models unexpectedly recover speaker identity in their deep layers
- Conducts large-scale probing analysis across 11 SSL models, decomposing speaker identity into acoustic, prosodic, and paralinguistic attributes
- Discovers that intermediate representations of speech SSL models capture dynamic prosody better than specialized speaker embeddings
- Confirms hierarchical encoding pattern: initial layers encode fundamental acoustics, middle layers synthesize abstract traits
- Provides guidelines for selecting interpretable and task-optimal representations based on internal mechanics analysis

### [Llama-Mimi: Exploring the Limits of Flattened Speech Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14882)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Llama-Mimi proposes flattening multi-level audio tokens from the Mimi neural codec into a single sequence and modeling them with a standard Transformer decoder, rather than using hierarchical architectures that explicitly capture the multi-level structure. This approach differs from prior work by adopting the NLP trend toward simpler, less architecturally biased models, moving away from hierarchical designs typically used for Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) tokens. The method outperforms hierarchical CSM-based models on most tasks and achieves the best acoustic consistency performance.

**Key points:**

- Llama-Mimi proposes flattening multi-level RVQ tokens from neural audio codecs into a single sequence, departing from traditional hierarchical architectures used in Speech Language Models
- The approach follows NLP trends toward simpler architectures by using a single Transformer decoder instead of complex hierarchical models to process multi-level audio representations
- Llama-Mimi outperforms CSM-based hierarchical models on most tasks and achieves best performance on acoustic consistency, demonstrating that architectural simplification can improve results
- The method uses autoregressive modeling with the Mimi neural audio codec, showing that flattened token sequences can effectively capture both semantic and acoustic information in speech
