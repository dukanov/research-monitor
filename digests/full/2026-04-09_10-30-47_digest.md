### [In-Context Learning in Speech Language Models: Analyzing the Role of Acoustic Features, Linguistic Structure, and Induction Heads](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06356)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper investigates in-context learning (ICL) in speech language models using text-to-speech tasks, analyzing how acoustic features (speaking rate, pitch, intensity) and linguistic structure affect both content generation accuracy and acoustic mimicry of demonstration speech. Unlike previous ICL research focused on text-only models, this work extends to the speech domain and finds that speaking rate strongly influences ICL performance and gets reproduced in outputs, while pitch and intensity have minimal impact. The authors demonstrate that induction heads play a causal role in speech-based ICL, similar to text models, as ablating top-k induction heads completely eliminates the model's ICL capabilities.

**Key points:**

- First comprehensive investigation of In-Context Learning (ICL) in speech domain, extending beyond extensively studied text-only language models to Speech Language Models
- Novel dual-angle analysis of speech ICL using Text-to-Speech task: evaluating both task inference accuracy (generating correct spoken content) and acoustic mimicking capability
- Key finding that speaking rate strongly affects ICL performance and is consistently mimicked in output, while pitch range and intensity show minimal impact on performance and inconsistent reproduction
- Causal role of induction heads in speech-based ICL demonstrated through ablation studies - removing top-k induction heads completely eliminates the model's ICL ability, mirroring findings from text-based ICL research

### [Mismatch Aware Guidance for Robust Emotion Control in Auto-Regressive TTS Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13293)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper tackles the problem of style-content mismatch in auto-regressive TTS models, where emotional prompts conflict with text semantics, leading to unnatural speech. The authors propose an adaptive Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scheme that dynamically adjusts guidance strength based on mismatch levels detected using large language models or natural language inference models. The key innovation is making CFG adaptive rather than fixed, specifically for auto-regressive TTS where CFG application has been underexplored, demonstrating improved emotional expressiveness while preserving audio quality.

**Key points:**

- Proposes adaptive Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) scheme that dynamically adjusts to different levels of style-content mismatch in auto-regressive TTS models, measured using large language models
- Addresses the underexplored challenge of applying CFG to auto-regressive TTS models, where traditional CFG can degrade audio quality
- Identifies and tackles the fundamental problem of style-content mismatch when desired emotion conflicts with semantic content in emotional TTS systems
- Demonstrates that adaptive CFG improves emotional expressiveness while maintaining audio quality and intelligibility, based on comprehensive analysis of CFG's impact on AR TTS models
