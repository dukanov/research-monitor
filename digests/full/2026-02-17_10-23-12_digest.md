### [GSRM: Generative Speech Reward Model for Speech RLHF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13891)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

The paper introduces GSRM (Generative Speech Reward Model), a reasoning-based evaluator for speech naturalness that decomposes evaluation into acoustic feature extraction followed by explainable chain-of-thought reasoning, rather than directly regressing audio to scores. Unlike existing naturalness evaluators that provide limited interpretability and poor cross-domain generalization, GSRM offers explainable judgments and generalizes across different speech taxonomies, achieving model-human correlation approaching human inter-rater consistency on a 31k expert ratings dataset. The model successfully serves as a verifier for reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to improve speech generation quality in language models.

**Key points:**

- Introduces GSRM, a reasoning-centric reward model that decomposes speech naturalness evaluation into interpretable acoustic feature extraction followed by feature-grounded chain-of-thought reasoning for explainable judgments
- Achieves model-human correlation of naturalness score prediction that approaches human inter-rater consistency, substantially outperforming existing speech naturalness predictors
- Unlike existing evaluators that regress raw audio to scalar scores with limited interpretability, GSRM provides explainable evaluations and generalizes across different speech taxonomies
- Demonstrates effectiveness as a verifier for online RLHF, successfully improving the naturalness of speech language model generations
- Trained on large-scale human feedback dataset with 31k expert ratings plus out-of-domain benchmark of real-world user-assistant speech interactions

### [Investigation for Relative Voice Impression Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14172)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces relative voice impression estimation (RIE), a framework that predicts perceptual differences between two utterances from the same speaker along semantic axes like "Dark-Bright" rather than providing absolute impression scores. The authors compare three approaches using a professional speaker's varied readings and find that self-supervised speech representations significantly outperform classical acoustic features, particularly for capturing complex impressions like "Cold-Warm." A notable limitation is that current multimodal large language models proved unreliable for this fine-grained pairwise comparison task.

**Key points:**

- Introduces relative voice impression estimation (RIE) - a novel framework for predicting perceptual differences between two utterances from the same speaker, rather than absolute impression scoring
- Self-supervised speech representations significantly outperform classical acoustic features, particularly for complex dynamic impressions like 'Cold-Warm' where classical features completely fail
- Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) prove unreliable for fine-grained pairwise voice impression tasks
- First systematic investigation of relative voice impression estimation, demonstrating the strength of self-supervised models in capturing subtle perceptual variations
- Uses low-dimensional vectors derived from subjective evaluations to quantify perceptual shifts along antonymic axes (e.g., 'Dark-Bright')

### [Disentangling Pitch and Creak for Speaker Identity Preservation in Speech Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14686)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents a speech synthesis system that can modify vocal creak (a voice quality characteristic) while preserving speaker identity by using conditional continuous normalizing flows to disentangle creak from pitch during training data augmentation. The key contribution is recognizing that while creak and low pitch are statistically correlated across speakers, they can be independently manipulated without losing speaker identity. The approach shows improved speaker verification performance across different levels of creak manipulation, though evaluation appears limited to this single metric.

**Key points:**

- Novel speech synthesis system that can modify creak (voice quality) while preserving speaker identity using conditional continuous normalizing flow with dataset augmentation
- Key insight that high creak probability correlates with low pitch at population level but not universally across all speaking situations
- Successfully disentangles pitch from creak through speaker manipulation block, addressing a challenging problem in voice quality modification
- Demonstrates improved speaker verification performance across various creak manipulation strengths, indicating effective identity preservation
