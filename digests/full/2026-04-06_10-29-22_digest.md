### [Borderless Long Speech Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19798)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces a unified framework for long-form speech synthesis that can handle complex scenarios like multi-speaker interactions, interruptions, and evolving emotional contexts by using hierarchical "Global-Sentence-Token" annotations and Chain-of-Thought reasoning. Unlike traditional TTS systems that synthesize sentence-by-sentence or from plain text alone, this approach maintains global context awareness and creates a structured interface between LLM agents and the synthesis engine, enabling control from high-level scene semantics down to phonetic details. The system extends beyond text-to-speech by accepting multimodal inputs through a front-end LLM that converts them into structured generation commands.

**Key points:**

- Introduces Borderless Long Speech Synthesis framework that captures global context, multi-speaker interactions (interruptions, overlapping speech), and evolving emotional arcs - moving beyond sentence-by-sentence synthesis
- Proposes 'Labeling over filtering/cleaning' data strategy with Global-Sentence-Token multi-level annotation schema for hierarchical speech control
- Incorporates Chain-of-Thought reasoning and Dimension Dropout to markedly improve instruction following under complex synthesis conditions
- Creates 'Native Agentic' design where hierarchical annotations serve as Structured Semantic Interface between LLM agents and synthesis engines
- Extends paradigm from Text2Speech to any-modality input synthesis by using text as information-complete, wide-band control channel for borderless long audio generation

### [DiFlowDubber: Discrete Flow Matching for Automated Video Dubbing via Cross-Modal Alignment and Synchronization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14267)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

DiFlowDubber introduces the first video dubbing framework based on discrete flow matching, using a two-stage training approach where a zero-shot TTS system is first pre-trained, then adapted for dubbing through cross-modal alignment and facial expression-conditioned prosody generation. The key innovation is combining a Synchronizer for lip synchronization with a Face-to-Prosody Mapper that extracts prosodic cues from facial expressions, enabling simultaneous optimization of content accuracy, prosody, acoustics, and lip sync. The framework demonstrates improvements over existing methods on benchmark datasets, though the abstract doesn't discuss computational requirements or real-time performance constraints.

**Key points:**

- First video dubbing framework built on discrete flow matching backbone with novel two-stage training strategy combining zero-shot TTS pre-training and Content-Consistent Temporal Adaptation (CCTA)
- Introduces Face-to-Prosody Mapper (FaPro) that conditions prosody generation on facial expressions, enabling cross-modal alignment between visual and audio features
- Discrete Flow-based Prosody-Acoustic (DFPA) module captures both expressive prosody and realistic acoustic characteristics through deterministic architecture
- Addresses four key dubbing challenges simultaneously: content accuracy, expressive prosody, high-quality acoustics, and precise lip synchronization where existing methods struggle
- Outperforms prior methods across multiple evaluation metrics on two benchmark datasets, demonstrating effectiveness of the multimodal embedding approach
