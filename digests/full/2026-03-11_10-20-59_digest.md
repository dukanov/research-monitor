### [VoxEmo: Benchmarking Speech Emotion Recognition with Speech LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08936)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

VoxEmo introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating Speech Large Language Models on emotion recognition, covering 35 emotion corpora across 15 languages with standardized evaluation protocols that account for the stochastic nature of text generation and human emotion ambiguity. Unlike existing benchmarks, it uses a distribution-aware soft-label protocol and prompt-ensemble strategy to better reflect real-world emotion perception, moving beyond simple closed-set classification. The main limitation is that zero-shot speech LLMs still underperform supervised baselines on hard-label accuracy, though they better align with human subjective emotion distributions.

**Key points:**

- VoxEmo introduces a comprehensive Speech Emotion Recognition benchmark with 35 emotion corpora across 15 languages specifically designed for Speech LLMs
- The benchmark addresses evaluation challenges by introducing a distribution-aware soft-label protocol and prompt-ensemble strategy that emulates annotator disagreement to reflect real-world emotion ambiguity
- Speech LLMs show lower hard-label accuracy compared to supervised baselines but uniquely align with human subjective emotion distributions
- The evaluation framework includes varying prompt complexities ranging from direct classification to paralinguistic reasoning tasks
- Zero-shot stochasticity in generative interfaces makes Speech LLM evaluation highly sensitive to prompt design, highlighting a key limitation in current approaches

### [EmoSURA: Towards Accurate Evaluation of Detailed and Long-Context Emotional Speech Captions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09820)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

EmoSURA introduces a novel evaluation framework for emotional speech captions that decomposes complex descriptions into "Atomic Perceptual Units" (self-contained statements about vocal/emotional attributes) and verifies each unit against the raw audio signal, rather than scoring captions holistically. This approach differs from existing methods by avoiding the semantic limitations of N-gram metrics and the reasoning inconsistencies of LLM judges when processing long-form descriptions. The authors also contribute SURABench, a standardized benchmark, and demonstrate positive correlation with human judgments where traditional metrics showed negative correlations due to length sensitivity.

**Key points:**

- EmoSURA introduces a paradigm shift from holistic scoring to atomic verification by decomposing complex captions into Atomic Perceptual Units (APUs) - self-contained statements about vocal/emotional attributes validated against raw speech signals
- Traditional N-gram metrics fail to capture semantic nuances while LLM judges suffer from reasoning inconsistency and context-collapse when processing long-form emotional speech descriptions
- EmoSURA achieves positive correlation with human judgments, while traditional metrics demonstrated negative correlations due to their sensitivity to caption length
- The work addresses evaluation resource scarcity by introducing SURABench, a carefully balanced and stratified benchmark for emotional speech caption evaluation

### [Universal Speech Content Factorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08977)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

USCF is a linear method that separates phonetic content from speaker timbre in speech by learning a universal speech-to-content mapping through least-squares optimization, requiring only seconds of target speaker data for voice conversion. Unlike previous closed-set approaches, it works in an open-set setting with unseen speakers and achieves competitive zero-shot voice conversion performance compared to methods requiring substantially more training data. The method also serves as an efficient timbre-disentangled feature for training text-to-speech models.

**Key points:**

- USCF extends closed-set Speech Content Factorization to open-set voice conversion using a simple invertible linear method that learns universal speech-to-content mapping via least-squares optimization
- The method requires only a few seconds of target speech to derive speaker-specific transformations, making it highly data-efficient compared to existing approaches
- USCF achieves competitive zero-shot voice conversion performance in intelligibility, naturalness, and speaker similarity without requiring substantial target-speaker data or additional neural training
- The approach effectively removes speaker-dependent variation while preserving phonetic content, as demonstrated through embedding analysis
- USCF features can serve as training-efficient timbre-disentangled acoustic representations for timbre-prompted text-to-speech model training

### [Textless and Non-Parallel Speech-to-Speech Emotion Style Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17655)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

The paper presents S2S-ZEST, a framework for transferring emotional style from a reference speech to a source speech while preserving the source's content and speaker identity, using an analysis-synthesis pipeline that decomposes speech into semantic tokens, speaker representations, and emotion embeddings. The key contribution is enabling zero-shot emotion transfer without requiring text transcriptions or parallel training data (same content spoken with different emotions), unlike existing methods. The authors demonstrate improved performance over prior approaches and show applicability for data augmentation in emotion recognition tasks.

**Key points:**

- Introduces S2S-ZEST, a novel zero-shot emotion style transfer framework that operates in textless and non-parallel settings, eliminating the need for text transcriptions or aligned speech pairs
- Proposes an analysis-synthesis pipeline that decomposes speech into semantic tokens, speaker representations, and emotion embeddings, enabling independent manipulation of emotional attributes while preserving content and speaker identity
- Achieves improved emotion style transfer performance compared to prior methods through auto-encoding training objective that enables efficient resynthesis during inference
- Demonstrates practical application for data augmentation in emotion recognition tasks, suggesting broader utility beyond style transfer
- Addresses key limitation of existing methods by enabling emotion transfer without requiring parallel training data or text supervision

### [VSSFlow: Unifying Video-conditioned Sound and Speech Generation via Joint Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24773)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

VSSFlow presents a unified flow-matching framework that jointly handles video-to-sound generation and visual text-to-speech synthesis using a Diffusion Transformer architecture with a novel disentangled condition aggregation mechanism (cross-attention for semantic conditions, self-attention for temporal conditions). Unlike previous work that treated these as separate tasks, VSSFlow demonstrates that joint training actually maintains superior performance rather than causing degradation, and can effectively adapt to combined sound and speech generation using synthetic data. The framework surpasses state-of-the-art domain-specific baselines on both individual tasks and joint generation benchmarks.

**Key points:**

- VSSFlow introduces the first unified flow-matching framework that seamlessly handles both Video-to-Sound (V2S) and Visual Text-to-Speech (VisualTTS) generation, bridging previously distinct tasks
- Novel disentangled condition aggregation mechanism leverages different attention properties: cross-attention for semantic conditions and self-attention for temporally-intensive conditions within a Diffusion Transformer architecture
- Contradicts prevailing belief by demonstrating that joint training for V2S and VisualTTS tasks maintains superior performance rather than causing degradation during end-to-end learning
- Employs straightforward feature-level data synthesis method that enables robust adaptation to joint sound and speech generation using synthetic data
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance across V2S, VisualTTS, and joint generation benchmarks, surpassing domain-specific baselines and highlighting the potential of unified generative models

### [Acoustic and Semantic Modeling of Emotion in Spoken Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09212)

**Key points:**

- Introduces speech-driven supervised pre-training framework that enables large-scale emotion-aware text modeling without requiring manually annotated text corpora, addressing data scarcity challenges
- Develops hierarchical architectures combining cross-modal attention and mixture-of-experts fusion for conversational emotion recognition, specifically designed to integrate information across conversational turns
- Presents textless and non-parallel speech-to-speech framework for emotion style transfer that preserves speaker identity and linguistic content while enabling controllable emotional transformations
- Demonstrates that style-transferred speech can serve as data augmentation to improve emotion recognition performance, providing both synthesis capabilities and practical applications for downstream tasks

The approach demonstrates strong transferability across different VL backbones while fully preserving their original vision-language performance.

**Key points:**

- The approach achieves performance comparable to omni-models trained on millions of hours of speech data while using only thousands of hours, demonstrating significant efficiency gains
- A novel QTATS (Question-Text Answer-Text-Speech) data construction strategy is proposed to generate training data from existing ASR speech-text pairs, addressing the scarcity of spoken QA corpora
- The learned speech modules exhibit strong transferability across different vision-language backbones while fully preserving the original VL performance
- The framework enables cost-effective multimodal capability extension without requiring massive computational resources or retraining of the entire model

### [Latent Speech-Text Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06195)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

**Latent Speech-Text Transformer (LST)** addresses the computational inefficiency of speech-text models by aggregating individual speech tokens into larger "latent speech patches" that serve as higher-level autoregressive units, reducing the sequence length imbalance between speech and text modalities. This approach improves both computational efficiency and cross-modal alignment, achieving up to +6.5% absolute improvement on speech HellaSwag while also improving text performance across scaling experiments from 420M to 7B parameters. The method maintains benefits in downstream ASR and TTS tasks by reducing effective sequence lengths during inference without degrading reconstruction quality.

**Key points:**

- LST introduces latent speech patches as higher-level autoregressive units to address the modality imbalance between speech and text tokens, aligning sequence-modeling granularity while improving computational efficiency
- The method achieves simultaneous improvements in both speech (+6.5% on speech HellaSwag) and text performance, demonstrating effective cross-modal knowledge transfer through patch-text alignment
- Performance gains scale with model size from 420M to 1.8B parameters in compute-optimal regimes and persist up to 7B parameters, suggesting the approach benefits from increased scale
- LST patches can compactly capture recurring acoustic patterns such as silence, providing a more efficient representation of speech compared to raw token sequences
- The approach reduces effective autoregressive sequence length during ASR and TTS inference, lowering computational cost without degrading reconstruction quality

### [HumeAI/tada-3b-ml](https://huggingface.co/HumeAI/tada-3b-ml)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

TADA is a unified speech-language model that achieves 1:1 alignment between text tokens and speech vectors, allowing it to generate speech and text in a synchronized stream. Unlike traditional TTS models that use fixed frame rates, TADA dynamically determines speech duration and prosody for each text token in a single autoregressive step, eliminating the computational overhead of fixed-frame-rate processing. The approach requires transcripts for non-English audio prompts since the built-in ASR is English-only.

**Key points:**

- TADA introduces 1:1 token alignment between text and audio, where each text token corresponds to exactly one speech vector, enabling synchronized speech-text streams unlike traditional fixed-frame-rate models
- Dynamic duration synthesis allows generating complete speech segments for text tokens in single autoregressive steps regardless of duration, eliminating the need for fixed audio frame rates (e.g., 50 frames/second)
- Dual-stream generation simultaneously produces text tokens and speech for preceding tokens, maintaining same context length as text-only models with minimal computational overhead
- The approach eliminates transcript hallucination issues common in traditional TTS models by breaking away from fixed-frame constraints and enabling more natural prosodic flow
- Multilingual capabilities span 9 languages (Arabic, Chinese, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Polish, Portuguese) with language-specific aligners, though non-English prompts require transcript input due to English-only ASR
