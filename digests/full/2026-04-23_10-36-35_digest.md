### [SpeechParaling-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Paralinguistic-Aware Speech Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20842)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces SpeechParaling-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating paralinguistic-aware speech generation in Large Audio-Language Models, expanding coverage from <50 to 100+ fine-grained features across three challenging tasks (fine-grained control, intra-utterance variation, and context-aware adaptation). The key innovation is using pairwise comparison with LALM-based judges rather than subjective human evaluation, making assessment more scalable and consistent. Experiments reveal significant limitations in current LALMs, with even leading models struggling with paralinguistic control and 43.3% of situational dialogue errors stemming from incorrect paralinguistic cue interpretation.

**Key points:**

- SpeechParaling-Bench expands paralinguistic feature coverage from fewer than 50 to over 100 fine-grained features with 1,000+ English-Chinese parallel speech queries across three progressively challenging tasks
- Novel pairwise comparison evaluation pipeline using LALM-based judge addresses subjectivity by framing assessment as relative preference rather than absolute scoring, eliminating need for costly human annotation
- Even leading proprietary LALMs struggle significantly with comprehensive static control and dynamic modulation of paralinguistic features, revealing substantial limitations in current models
- 43.3% of errors in situational dialogue stem from failure to correctly interpret paralinguistic cues, highlighting critical gap in human-aligned voice assistant development
- The benchmark's three-tiered task structure (fine-grained control, intra-utterance variation, context-aware adaptation) provides progressive difficulty levels for comprehensive paralinguistic modeling assessment

### [WavAlign: Enhancing Intelligence and Expressiveness in Spoken Dialogue Models via Adaptive Hybrid Post-Training](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.14932)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper addresses the challenge of applying reinforcement learning to end-to-end spoken dialogue models by proposing WavAlign, a modality-aware post-training approach that separates semantic and acoustic optimization channels. Unlike direct preference optimization which struggles with sparse supervision over dense speech generation, their method constrains preference updates to semantic content while using explicit anchoring for acoustic behavior, dynamically balancing these components based on rollout statistics. The approach shows consistent improvements in both semantic quality and speech expressiveness across multiple benchmarks and architectures.

**Key points:**

- Identifies that directly applying preference optimization/RL to spoken dialogue models is non-trivial due to sparse preference supervision interacting with dense speech generation under shared-parameter updates
- Proposes WavAlign: a modality-aware adaptive post-training recipe that constrains preference updates to semantic channels while improving acoustics via explicit anchoring
- Introduces dynamic regulation of semantic and acoustic mixture based on rollout statistics to avoid unreliable preference gradients
- Demonstrates consistent improvements in both semantic quality and speech expressiveness across multiple spoken dialogue benchmarks and architectures
- Addresses the gap between the theoretical potential of end-to-end spoken dialogue models and the disappointing performance of current open-source implementations

### [MoVE: Translating Laughter and Tears via Mixture of Vocalization Experts in Speech-to-Speech Translation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.17435)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper addresses a key limitation in speech-to-speech translation systems that lose non-verbal vocalizations like laughter and crying during translation. The authors propose MoVE, a Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts architecture that uses specialized adapters and soft routing to preserve expressive elements, achieving 76% reproduction of target non-verbal vocalizations compared to existing systems' 14%. The approach demonstrates remarkable data efficiency, requiring only 30 minutes of curated expressive data when leveraging pretrained AudioLLMs, though evaluation is limited to English-Chinese translation.

**Key points:**

- MoVE introduces a Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts architecture with expressive-specialized adapters and soft-weighting router to preserve non-verbal vocalizations (laughter, crying) in speech-to-speech translation, addressing a critical limitation where existing systems strip away pragmatic intent
- The system achieves remarkable improvement in preserving target non-verbal vocalizations, reproducing them in 76% of cases compared to existing S2ST systems that preserve at most 14%
- Pretrained AudioLLMs enable striking data efficiency, requiring only 30 minutes of curated expressive data to achieve strong performance in capturing hybrid expressive states
- A novel synthesis pipeline is proposed to build scalable expressive datasets, overcoming the fundamental data scarcity limitation in expressive speech-to-speech translation
- MoVE achieves highest human-rated naturalness and emotional fidelity among compared systems, demonstrating that preserving non-verbal elements significantly enhances real-world utility of S2ST systems
