### [TADA: A Generative Framework for Speech Modeling via Text-Acoustic Dual Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23068)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

TADA introduces a novel tokenization scheme that creates one-to-one synchronization between acoustic features and text tokens, enabling unified speech modeling within a single LLM stream using flow matching for generation. Unlike existing TTS systems that use fixed-frame-rate acoustic tokenization (creating much longer, asynchronous speech sequences), this approach eliminates the sequence length disparity and introduces "text-only guidance" that blends text-only and text-speech mode logits to bridge toward text-only LLM intelligence. The method reportedly achieves competitive performance while reducing content hallucinations and inference costs, though the work appears to be future-dated (2026) which may indicate preliminary or hypothetical results.

**Key points:**

- Proposes novel tokenization scheme achieving one-to-one synchronization between continuous acoustic features and text tokens, eliminating the typical sequence length disparity in TTS systems
- Introduces 'text-only guidance' technique that blends logits from text-only and text-speech modes to bridge the gap toward text-only LLM intelligence
- Uses flow matching head with LLM architecture for unified, single-stream modeling of synchronous speech-text tokens in latent space
- Achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art TTS and SLM systems while virtually eliminating content hallucinations and significantly reducing inference cost
- Addresses key limitation of existing LLM-based TTS systems where fixed-frame-rate acoustic tokenization creates asynchronous, computationally inefficient speech sequences

### [LibriTTS-VI: A Public Corpus and Novel Methods for Efficient Voice Impression Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15626)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper addresses voice impression control in text-to-speech synthesis by proposing methods to mitigate "impression leakage" - where synthesized voices are undesirably influenced by reference audio rather than the intended target impression (e.g., brighter, calmer). The authors introduce two novel approaches: a training strategy that separates speaker identity and impression using different utterances, and a reference-free model that generates speaker embeddings directly from target impressions, achieving better controllability while reducing mean squared error from 0.61 to 0.41 objectively. They also contribute LibriTTS-VI, the first public voice impression dataset with standardized annotations built on LibriTTS-R corpus.

**Key points:**

- Identified and addressed 'impression leakage' problem where synthesized voice is undesirably influenced by reference audio rather than target impression specifications
- Proposed two novel methods: training strategy using separate utterances for speaker identity vs target impression, and reference-free model generating embeddings solely from target impression
- Achieved significant improvement in controllability, reducing MSE of 11-dimensional voice impression vectors from 0.61 to 0.41 objectively and 1.15 to 0.92 subjectively while maintaining high fidelity
- Released LibriTTS-VI, the first public voice impression dataset with clear annotation standards built on LibriTTS-R corpus to enable reproducible research
- Reference-free approach provides dual benefits of improved robustness against impression leakage and convenience of not requiring reference audio for generation
