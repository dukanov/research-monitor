### [DARS: Dysarthria-Aware Rhythm-Style Synthesis for ASR Enhancement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01369)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

DARS proposes a specialized text-to-speech framework that generates synthetic dysarthric speech for ASR data augmentation, using a multi-stage rhythm predictor with contrastive learning between normal and dysarthric speech patterns, plus dysarthric-style conditional flow matching to capture pathological acoustic characteristics. Unlike existing TTS augmentation methods, DARS specifically models the abnormal prosody and rhythm patterns characteristic of dysarthric speech rather than treating it as generic speech variation. The evaluation is limited to the TORGO dataset, though results show substantial ASR improvement (54% relative WER reduction) when using the synthetic dysarthric data for model adaptation.

**Key points:**

- DARS introduces a novel dysarthria-aware synthesis framework using Matcha-TTS architecture with multi-stage rhythm predictor and contrastive preferences between normal and dysarthric speech patterns
- The framework addresses limitations of existing TTS-based data augmentation methods that fail to accurately model pathological rhythm and acoustic style of dysarthric speech
- DARS achieves Mean Cepstral Distortion of 4.29, closely approximating real dysarthric speech quality on the TORGO dataset
- ASR system adaptation with DARS-generated synthetic dysarthric speech achieves 54.22% relative word error rate reduction compared to state-of-the-art methods
- The approach jointly enhances temporal rhythm reconstruction and pathological acoustic style simulation through dysarthric-style conditional flow matching

### [S-VoCAL: A Dataset and Evaluation Framework for Inferring Speaking Voice Character Attributes in Literature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00958)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

The paper introduces S-VoCAL, the first dataset and evaluation framework for inferring voice-related character attributes (age, gender, origin, physical health, etc.) from literary texts, aimed at improving synthetic audiobook narration by assigning appropriate voices to fictional characters. The dataset contains 952 character-book pairs from Project Gutenberg with 8 sociophonetically-grounded attributes and uses a novel LLM embedding-based similarity metric for evaluation. Initial experiments with a RAG pipeline show reliable performance on basic attributes like age and gender but poor performance on more complex attributes like origin and physical health.

**Key points:**

- Introduces S-VoCAL, the first dataset and evaluation framework specifically designed for inferring voice-related fictional character attributes from literature, addressing a gap in synthetic audiobook narration systems
- Created a comprehensive dataset with 8 voice attributes grounded in sociophonetic studies and 952 character-book pairs from Project Gutenberg, with a novel similarity metric based on Large Language Model embeddings
- Demonstrates that simple Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline reliably infers basic attributes like Age and Gender but struggles with more complex attributes like Origin and Physical Health
- Addresses the challenge of character voice assignment in synthetic audiobook narration, which requires complex inference of attributes across book-length contexts for enhanced character identification
