### [ParaS2S: Benchmarking and Aligning Spoken Language Models for Paralinguistic-aware Speech-to-Speech Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08723)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

This paper introduces ParaS2S, a reinforcement learning framework for training speech-to-speech models to handle paralinguistic cues (emotion, tone, speaker attributes) and respond appropriately in both content and speaking style at the waveform level. The key innovation is combining a new benchmark (ParaS2SBench) with an automatic judge using "PolyTone training" to prevent style hallucination, enabling RL-based training that achieves 10% relative improvement over supervised fine-tuning while requiring fewer paired demonstrations. The work reveals that existing S2S models perform poorly on paralinguistic-aware tasks, often no better than pipeline baselines, highlighting the need for specialized approaches to handle expressive speech interaction.

**Key points:**

- Introduces ParaS2S, a novel reinforcement learning framework that evaluates and optimizes speech-to-speech models for paralinguistic cues (emotion, tone, speaker attributes) directly at the waveform level
- Proposes PolyTone training strategy and multi-stage framework to prevent style hallucination in end-to-end audio LLM judging, creating an automatic evaluator that correlates well with human preferences
- Reveals that existing S2S models fail to respond appropriately to paralinguistic attributes, performing no better than pipeline-based baselines on the new ParaS2SBench benchmark
- Demonstrates that the RL approach (ParaS2SAlign) achieves 10% relative improvement over supervised fine-tuning while requiring substantially fewer paired demonstrations
- Highlights the critical need for scalable automatic evaluation methods in speech-to-speech interaction due to the scarcity of high-quality expressive demonstrations

### [Prosodic Boundary-Aware Streaming Generation for LLM-Based TTS with Streaming Text Input](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06444)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents a method for streaming text-to-speech that can handle incrementally received text input by training an LLM-based TTS model to stop at prosodic boundaries when given limited future context, combined with a sliding-window approach to maintain bounded context during long-form synthesis. The key innovation is the prosodic-boundary-aware post-training strategy that addresses two major challenges: unnatural prosody from missing lookahead text and performance collapse from unbounded context accumulation. The method shows substantial improvements over existing approaches, particularly for long-form text where it reduces word error rate from 71.0% to 4.8%, though it requires weakly time-aligned training data and a pretrained LLM-based TTS model as prerequisites.

**Key points:**

- Novel prosodic boundary-aware post-training strategy that adapts pretrained LLM-based TTS models to learn early stopping at content boundaries using weakly time-aligned data
- Addresses two critical streaming TTS challenges: unnatural prosody from missing lookahead context and long-form collapse from unbounded context accumulation
- Achieves dramatic improvement in long-text synthesis with 66.2% absolute reduction in word error rate (from 71.0% to 4.8%) and significant gains in speaker similarity (16.1%) and emotion similarity (1.5%)
- Introduces sliding-window prompt mechanism that carries forward previous text and speech tokens to ensure bounded context while maintaining seamless audio concatenation
- Outperforms CosyVoice-Style interleaved baseline across both short-form and long-form synthesis scenarios, demonstrating robust streaming TTS with incremental text input

### [Efficient Emotion and Speaker Adaptation in LLM-Based TTS via Characteristic-Specific Partial Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14273)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces CSP-FT (characteristic-specific partial fine-tuning), which selectively fine-tunes only the two layers that capture the most and least emotion/speaker information in LLM-based TTS models, rather than uniformly fine-tuning all parameters. The approach differs from existing work by using dynamic layer contribution analysis to target specific parameters, updating only ~8% of the model while achieving comparable fidelity and intelligibility to full fine-tuning with 2x faster training and reduced catastrophic forgetting. The method was validated across 11 datasets, showing effective adaptation for unseen emotion and speaker domains.

**Key points:**

- CSP-FT proposes characteristic-specific partial fine-tuning that dynamically analyzes layer contributions via weighted sum to selectively fine-tune only the two layers capturing most and least emotion/speaker information
- Achieves comparable or better performance to full fine-tuning while updating only ~8% of parameters and accelerating training by ~2x
- Addresses key limitations of uniform fine-tuning in LLM-based TTS: slow training, catastrophic forgetting, and degraded pronunciation accuracy on limited data
- Significantly mitigates catastrophic forgetting compared to standard fine-tuning approaches while maintaining cloning fidelity and intelligibility
- Validated on combined corpus of 11 datasets, demonstrating robust performance across diverse domains

### [Reconstruct! Don't Encode: Self-Supervised Representation Reconstruction Loss for High-Intelligibility and Low-Latency Streaming Neural Audio Codec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05887)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces self-supervised representation reconstruction (SSRR) loss for training neural audio codecs, where the codec learns to reconstruct distilled self-supervised speech representations from its outputs rather than just optimizing mel-spectrogram reconstruction. Unlike previous semantic encoder distillation approaches that don't guarantee content preservation, SSRR directly ensures intelligibility is maintained in the reconstructed speech while enabling zero-lookahead streaming architectures for real-time deployment. The method achieves faster convergence (single GPU training) and state-of-the-art performance with minimal latency, though the abstract doesn't mention specific limitations.

**Key points:**

- Self-supervised representation reconstruction (SSRR) loss fundamentally improves neural audio codec training by enhancing intelligibility through reconstructing distilled self-supervised representations from codec outputs
- SSRR significantly accelerates convergence, enabling competitive results using only a single GPU with reduced training cost
- The approach enables high intelligibility without additional lookahead in streaming Transformer-based codecs, allowing zero-lookahead architecture for real-time deployment
- JHCodec achieves state-of-the-art performance while maintaining minimal latency, addressing the key limitation of existing codecs optimized for mel-spectrogram reconstruction that fail to preserve intelligibility

### [Activation Steering for Accent-Neutralized Zero-Shot Text-To-Speech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05977)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents a training-free method to neutralize accents in zero-shot text-to-speech while preserving speaker timbre, using "activation steering vectors" derived from internal model activation differences between accented and native speech. The key innovation is that these steering vectors are extracted offline and applied during inference without requiring model retraining, unlike existing approaches that struggle to disentangle accent from voice characteristics. The method shows good generalization to unseen accented speakers, though the approach requires access to paired accented/native speech data to extract the steering vectors.

**Key points:**

- Introduces a novel training-free approach for accent neutralization in zero-shot TTS using inference-time activation steering vectors
- Successfully disentangles accent from voice timbre, addressing a key challenge where existing models inherit both attributes from reference speakers
- Steering vectors are extracted offline from internal activation differences between accented and native speech within the TTS model
- Demonstrates strong generalizability to unseen accented speakers while effectively preserving original speaker timbre
- Provides a practical post-hoc solution for accent-free voice cloning without requiring model retraining

### [StreamVoiceAnon+: Emotion-Preserving Streaming Speaker Anonymization via Frame-Level Acoustic Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06079)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper tackles streaming speaker anonymization while preserving emotional content through supervised finetuning with neutral-emotion utterance pairs and frame-level emotion distillation on acoustic token hidden states. The key innovation is addressing the common problem where neural audio codec language models degrade source emotions during anonymization, achieving a 24% relative improvement in emotion preservation (49.2% UAR) over the baseline while maintaining strong privacy protection (49.0% EER) and low latency (180ms). The approach requires neutral-emotion training pairs from the same speakers, which could limit practical applicability depending on data availability.

**Key points:**

- Novel frame-level acoustic distillation approach for emotion-preserving speaker anonymization, using supervised finetuning with neutral-emotion utterance pairs from the same speaker
- Achieves 49.2% UAR (emotion preservation) with significant improvements: +24% relative improvement over baseline (39.7%→49.2%) and +10% over emotion-prompt variant
- Identifies key limitation of neural audio codec language models: content tokens discard emotional information and models default to dominant acoustic patterns rather than preserving paralinguistic attributes
- Efficient implementation with zero inference latency overhead, maintaining competitive 180ms streaming latency while requiring less than 2 hours training on 4 GPUs
- Maintains strong privacy protection (EER 49.0%) and intelligibility (5.77% WER) while significantly improving emotion preservation
