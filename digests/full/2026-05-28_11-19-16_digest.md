### [Bridging the Stability-Expressivity Gap: Synthetic Data Scaling and Preference Alignment for Low-Resource Spoken Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27383)

**Relevance:** 80.0%

This paper addresses a fundamental trade-off in spoken language models for low-resource languages, termed the "Stability-Expressivity Gap," where synthetic data improves phonetic accuracy but reduces prosodic variability, leading to monotonous speech output. The authors propose two self-alignment frameworks - Disentanglement-Guided Self-Alignment (DGSA) that separates prosody and timbre for complex languages, and Temperature-Driven Self-Critique (TDSC) for extremely data-limited scenarios - to recover expressivity without requiring additional real speech data. The approach reportedly outperforms commercial systems like ElevenLabs and enables zero-shot voice cloning for Lao, though the method appears specifically designed for low-resource settings and may have limited applicability to well-resourced languages.

**Key points:**

- Identifies the 'Stability-Expressivity Gap': synthetic data improves phonetic accuracy but progressively suppresses prosodic variability, leading to 'Synthetic Erosion' and collapse of expressivity
- Proposes two novel self-alignment frameworks: Disentanglement-Guided Self-Alignment (DGSA) for complex languages using prosody-timbre separation, and Temperature-Driven Self-Critique (TDSC) for extremely limited data scenarios
- Outperforms strong commercial systems including ElevenLabs and Gemini Pro, demonstrating practical effectiveness of the proposed approach
- Enables the first zero-shot voice cloning capability for Lao, showcasing breakthrough performance in low-resource language synthesis

### [Do Audio LLMs Listen or Read? Analyzing and Mitigating Paralinguistic Failures with VoxParadox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27772)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

This paper introduces VoxParadox, an adversarial benchmark that tests whether Audio LLMs actually understand paralinguistic cues (tone, emotion, speaking style) from speech or just rely on transcript content, revealing that current models perform poorly (17-37% accuracy) and tend to follow language-implied rather than acoustically-supported answers. The key innovation is using controlled speech synthesis to create examples where transcript claims intentionally mismatch speaking style, plus proposing technical solutions (Prompt-Conditioned Layer Mixer and Direct Preference Optimization) that substantially improve performance to 54-65% accuracy. The work systematically diagnoses where paralinguistic information degrades in Audio LLM architectures through layer-wise probing, showing failures both in audio encoding and language model processing stages.

**Key points:**

- Introduced VoxParadox, an adversarial benchmark with 2,000 examples that uses controlled speech synthesis to intentionally mismatch transcript claims and speaking style, enabling direct measurement of paralinguistic understanding in Audio LLMs
- Found that Audio LLMs consistently show low accuracy on acoustic ground truth and have a strong tendency to follow language-implied (incorrect) answers rather than actual speech cues
- Discovered through layer-wise probing that paralinguistic cues degrade in deeper encoder layers and at the encoder-LLM interface, and that language models frequently ignore available acoustic information
- Proposed Prompt-Conditioned Layer Mixer (PCLM) combined with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to improve paralinguistic understanding, achieving substantial improvements from 17.40% to 65.20% on VoxParadox for Audio Flamingo 3
- Revealed a fundamental limitation where Audio LLMs appear to 'read' rather than truly 'listen', relying more on textual information than acoustic paralinguistic features

### [Comprehensive Benchmarking of Long-Form Speech Generation in Diverse Scenarios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28618)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

**Main approach**: The authors propose SwanBench-Speech, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating long-form speech generation that includes 1,101 samples across 17 scenarios and uses 7 automated metrics to assess acoustics, semantics, and expressiveness dimensions.

**Key difference**: Unlike existing benchmarks that focus on limited domains and fail to capture long-form factors like consistency and coherence, this benchmark specifically targets diverse real-world speech scenarios with systematic evaluation of long-context speech generation quality.

**Notable finding**: Their experiments reveal that current speech generation models still struggle significantly with highly expressive scenarios and show substantial gaps in consistency and hierarchical structure compared to real human recordings.

**Key points:**

- SwanBench-Speech introduces a comprehensive benchmark for long-form speech generation with 1,101 samples across 17 scenarios, addressing the gap in existing limited-domain test scenarios
- The benchmark decomposes speech quality into disentangled dimensions with automated evaluation protocol using seven metrics across acoustics, semantics, and expressiveness axes
- Current speech generation models exhibit significant struggles in highly expressive scenarios and show notable gaps in consistency and hierarchy compared to real recordings
- Existing evaluation metrics fail to capture critical long-text factors such as consistency and coherence, limiting their reliability for long-form speech assessment

### [Unlocking Fine-Grained and Within-Utterance Speaking Style Control in Prompt-Based Text-to-Speech Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27376)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces techniques to enable fine-grained speaking style control in prompt-based TTS models, addressing two key limitations: the ability to smoothly interpolate between different styles across utterances and to transition between styles within a single utterance. For inter-utterance interpolation, they compute direction vectors between contrastive style prompts in embedding space, while for intra-utterance transitions, they tackle the attention bias toward early tokens using KV-cache swapping and sliding-window attention masking. The approach demonstrates strong quantitative results (99-100% gender conversion success, significant pitch/speed variations) while maintaining speaker similarity and perceptual smoothness, representing an advance over existing prompt-based TTS that typically applies uniform styles across entire utterances.

**Key points:**

- Introduces direction vector interpolation between contrastive style prompts in embedding space to enable smooth inter-utterance style transitions, achieving 99-100% success rate in gender conversion with up to 36 Hz pitch variation
- Identifies and addresses strong attention bias toward early tokens in autoregressive TTS decoders that causes initial audio realization to dominate subsequent generation
- Proposes KV-cache swapping and sliding-window attention masking techniques to enable within-utterance style transitions while maintaining speaker similarity of 0.81-0.91
- Overcomes limitations of existing prompt-based TTS models that apply single global styles across utterances and lack fine-grained control capabilities
- Demonstrates significant style control improvements including up to 1.6 syllables-per-second speed changes and perceptual smoothness scores of 3.48-4.48 for intra-utterance transitions

### [zeropointnine/Darwin-TTS-1.7B-Cross-Qwen3Tokenizer](https://huggingface.co/zeropointnine/Darwin-TTS-1.7B-Cross-Qwen3Tokenizer)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This work presents a cross-modal approach to enhance text-to-speech (TTS) with emotional expressiveness by blending 3% of feed-forward network weights from a language model (Qwen3-1.7B) into a TTS model (Qwen3-TTS), using simple weight arithmetic without any training. The key innovation is leveraging identical architectures between the LLM and TTS model to enable direct weight transfer, claiming to add emotion to speech synthesis without requiring emotional speech datasets or training time. The approach is highly sensitive to blend ratios (>10% causes model failure) and requires architecturally identical base models, limiting its generalizability across different model families.

**Key points:**

- World's first cross-modal FFN transfer from LLM to TTS achieving emotion-enhanced speech synthesis without any training, using only weight-space arithmetic
- Discovery of optimal 3% blend ratio sweet spot where emotion appears while maintaining quality, with 10%+ ratios breaking TTS functionality by causing infinite generation
- Architecture compatibility requirement: successful transfer only works with 100% identical model structures (Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-TTS-1.7B share identical hidden_size, layers, attention heads)
- TTS models are far more sensitive to weight blending than LLMs - while LLM merging tolerates 7-93% ratios, TTS requires precise 3-5% for stability
- Cross-modal potential for bidirectional transfer suggested, with LLM + TTS FFN blending potentially enabling 'Speaking LLM' capabilities similar to GPT-4o direction
