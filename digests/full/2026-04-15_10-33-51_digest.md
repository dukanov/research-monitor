### [ZipVoice-Dialog: Non-Autoregressive Spoken Dialogue Generation with Flow Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09318)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

ZipVoice-Dialog presents a non-autoregressive flow-matching model for generating spoken dialogues with realistic turn-taking and distinct speaker voices, addressing the high latency and stability issues of existing autoregressive approaches. The key innovations include curriculum learning for robust speech-text alignment and speaker-turn embeddings for precise turn-taking control, plus they introduce OpenDialog, a 6.8k-hour open-source dialogue dataset. The authors had to develop these specialized techniques because vanilla flow-matching produced poor speech intelligibility and imprecise turn-taking when applied directly to dialogue generation.

**Key points:**

- Introduces ZipVoice-Dialog, a non-autoregressive flow-matching model that overcomes high inference latency and stability issues of autoregressive spoken dialogue systems
- Develops two key technical innovations: curriculum learning for robust speech-text alignment and speaker-turn embeddings for precise turn-taking control in flow-matching architectures
- Creates and releases OpenDialog, the first large-scale open-source spoken dialogue dataset with 6.8k hours of in-the-wild speech data
- Demonstrates that vanilla flow-matching fails for dialogue generation due to poor speech intelligibility and turn-taking precision, requiring specialized adaptations
- Achieves superior performance in inference speed, intelligibility, speaker turn-taking accuracy, and speaker similarity compared to existing autoregressive models

### [CoSyncDiT: Cognitive Synchronous Diffusion Transformer for Movie Dubbing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12292)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents CoSyncDiT, a flow matching-based diffusion transformer for movie dubbing that synthesizes speech matching a reference voice while synchronizing with target video lip movements. The approach differs from existing methods by using a three-stage cognitive process-inspired architecture (acoustic style adapting, visual calibrating, context aligning) combined with Joint Semantic and Alignment Regularization to avoid the interference issues that plague current implicit alignment methods. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on both standard and challenging in-the-wild dubbing benchmarks across multiple metrics.

**Key points:**

- CoSyncDiT introduces a novel flow matching-based movie dubbing framework using a Cognitive Synchronous Diffusion Transformer inspired by professional actors' cognitive processes
- Addresses key limitations of existing methods: lack of precise lip-sync, poor naturalness from explicit duration-level alignment, and timbre/pronunciation degradation from reference audio interference in implicit alignment solutions
- Employs a three-stage progressive guidance approach: acoustic style adapting, fine-grained visual calibrating, and time-aware context aligning to guide noise-to-speech generation
- Introduces Joint Semantic and Alignment Regularization (JSAR) mechanism that simultaneously constrains frame-level temporal consistency and semantic consistency on flow hidden states for robust alignment
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on both standard benchmarks and challenging in-the-wild dubbing scenarios across multiple evaluation metrics

### [Beyond Transcription: Unified Audio Schema for Perception-Aware AudioLLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12506)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper addresses a key weakness in Audio Large Language Models (AudioLLMs): while they excel at complex reasoning, they perform poorly on fine-grained acoustic perception tasks. The authors propose Unified Audio Schema (UAS), which structures audio supervision into three explicit components—Transcription, Paralinguistics, and Non-linguistic Events—within a unified JSON format, rather than using traditional ASR-centric training that treats non-linguistic audio as noise. Experiments show UAS improves fine-grained perception by 10.9% while preserving reasoning capabilities, suggesting this structured supervision approach better captures comprehensive acoustic information.

**Key points:**

- AudioLLMs exhibit performance inversion: excelling at complex reasoning tasks while consistently underperforming on fine-grained acoustic perception due to ASR-centric training that suppresses paralinguistic cues
- Unified Audio Schema (UAS) organizes audio information into three explicit components - Transcription, Paralinguistics, and Non-linguistic Events - within a unified JSON format to achieve comprehensive acoustic coverage
- UAS addresses the fundamental limitation of traditional training by providing structured supervision that maintains tight audio-text alignment while preserving acoustic details rather than treating them as noise
- Experimental validation shows UAS-Audio yields 10.9% improvement in fine-grained perception on MMSU over state-of-the-art models while preserving robust reasoning capabilities
- The approach demonstrates effectiveness across both discrete and continuous AudioLLM architectures, suggesting broad applicability of the unified schema framework

### [FINAL-Bench/Darwin-TTS-1.7B-Cross](https://huggingface.co/FINAL-Bench/Darwin-TTS-1.7B-Cross)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Darwin-TTS demonstrates cross-modal capability transfer by blending 3% of feed-forward network weights from a large language model (Qwen3-1.7B) into a text-to-speech model (Qwen3-TTS-1.7B) without training, reportedly adding emotional expressiveness to speech synthesis. The key requirement is identical transformer architectures between source and target models, enabling direct weight interpolation across 84 FFN tensors using simple linear blending. The approach appears quite brittle - requiring exact architectural matches and having a narrow tolerance window (10%+ blending ratios completely break TTS functionality).

**Key points:**

- World's first cross-modal FFN transfer from LLM to TTS achieving emotion-enhanced speech synthesis without any training, using only 3% weight blending between architecturally identical models
- Critical discovery that 3-5% blend ratio is optimal for TTS - below 1% shows no effect, while 10%+ completely breaks TTS by causing infinite generation as LLM's 'continue generating' pattern overrides TTS stop signals
- Success depends on identical backbone architectures (Qwen3-1.7B LLM and Qwen3-TTS talker share 100% identical parameters: 2048 hidden size, 28 layers, 16 attention heads) - different backbones (TADA-1B/Llama × Qwen3-TTS) failed completely
- TTS systems are far more sensitive to weight modifications than LLMs (which tolerate 7-93% blending ratios), requiring precise low-ratio blending to preserve audio codec pipeline integrity
- Only 8.6% of total model weights (84/976 FFN tensors) are modified while preserving all modality-specific components (speech tokenizer, encoder/decoder, code predictor), enabling cross-modal capability transfer through targeted architectural intervention
