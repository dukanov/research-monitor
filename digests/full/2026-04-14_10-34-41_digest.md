### [Sign-to-Speech Prosody Transfer via Sign Reconstruction-based GAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10413)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

This paper proposes "Sign-to-Speech Prosody Transfer" - directly transferring emotional and prosodic information from sign language into synthesized speech, bypassing the traditional sign→text→speech pipeline that loses non-verbal expressiveness. The key innovation is SignRecGAN, a training framework that learns cross-modal prosody transfer using only unimodal datasets (separate sign and speech data) without requiring expensive parallel sign-speech annotations, achieved through adversarial learning and reconstruction losses. The main challenge they address is the typical bottleneck of text representation that strips away the rich prosodic information originally present in signing.

**Key points:**

- Introduces novel Sign-to-Speech Prosody Transfer task that directly captures prosodic nuances from sign language and integrates them into synthesized speech, bypassing text bottleneck that loses non-verbal information
- Proposes SignRecGAN framework that overcomes the costly annotation problem by using adversarial learning with unimodal datasets instead of requiring expensive cross-modal parallel corpora
- Develops S2PFormer architecture that preserves existing TTS model capabilities while enabling injection of sign-derived prosody into speech synthesis
- Demonstrates ability to synthesize speech that faithfully reflects emotional content of sign language, opening new possibilities for more natural sign language communication

### [Bridging What the Model Thinks and How It Speaks: Self-Aware Speech Language Models for Expressive Speech Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11424)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

This paper addresses the "semantic understanding-acoustic realization gap" in Speech Language Models, where models understand content but generate flat, inexpressive speech. The authors propose SA-SLM, which uses two key mechanisms: Intent-Aware Bridging (converting internal semantics to expressive intent via Variational Information Bottleneck) and Realization-Aware Alignment (using the model as its own critic to verify acoustic output matches intended expression). Their 3B parameter model, trained on only 800 hours of expressive speech data, achieves performance close to GPT-4o-Audio on expressiveness benchmarks, with the relatively small training dataset being a potential limitation.

**Key points:**

- Identifies the 'semantic understanding-acoustic realization gap' where Speech Language Models understand semantics well but generate flat, unexpressive speech that fails to convey expressive intent
- Proposes SA-SLM with Intent-Aware Bridging using Variational Information Bottleneck to translate internal semantics into temporally smooth expressive intent
- Introduces Realization-Aware Alignment where the model serves as its own critic to verify acoustic outputs match intended expression via rubric-based feedback
- Achieves performance within 0.08 points of GPT-4o-Audio on EchoMind benchmark using only a 3B parameter model trained on 800 hours of expressive speech data

### [MimicLM: Zero-Shot Voice Imitation through Autoregressive Modeling of Pseudo-Parallel Speech Corpora](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11552)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

MimicLM addresses voice imitation by training on pseudo-parallel data where synthetic speech serves as the source and real recordings as targets, allowing the model to learn from real speech distributions rather than being limited by synthetic speech quality. This approach differs from existing methods that either use complex disentanglement architectures or suffer from quality ceilings when using synthetic speech as training targets. The method incorporates interleaved text-audio modeling and preference alignment to handle the distributional mismatch between synthetic training sources and real targets, though this mismatch remains an inherent challenge.

**Key points:**

- MimicLM introduces a novel approach using synthetic speech as training sources while retaining real recordings as targets, breaking the synthetic quality ceiling that limits existing methods
- The method incorporates interleaved text-audio modeling to guide content-accurate speech generation and applies post-training with preference alignment to address distributional mismatch
- Unlike prior work requiring complex disentanglement architectures or suffering from synthetic target quality limitations, MimicLM achieves superior results with a simple yet effective architecture
- Experimental results show significant improvements in naturalness while maintaining competitive similarity scores across speaker identity, accent, and emotion dimensions
- The approach enables learning directly from real speech distributions rather than being constrained by synthetic speech quality as training targets

### [Knowing What to Stress: A Discourse-Conditioned Text-to-Speech Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10580)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces CAST, a benchmark for evaluating whether text-to-speech systems can infer contextually appropriate word-level stress from discourse context using contrastive sentence pairs that require different emphasis patterns. The key finding is that while text-only language models successfully identify which words should be stressed based on context, current TTS systems fail to realize this stress in the actual speech output, revealing a significant gap between contextual understanding and speech production. The benchmark addresses an underexplored aspect of TTS evaluation by specifically testing discourse-conditioned stress placement rather than general expressiveness.

**Key points:**

- Introduces CAST (Context-Aware Stress TTS), a novel benchmark using contrastive context pairs to evaluate whether TTS systems can infer contextually appropriate word-level stress from discourse alone
- Reveals a significant performance gap: text-only language models reliably recover intended stress from context, while state-of-the-art TTS systems frequently fail to realize this stress in synthesized speech
- Addresses an understudied problem in TTS evaluation - how the same sentence can convey different meanings (correction, contrast, clarification) depending on which word receives emphasis
- Provides comprehensive resources including benchmark dataset, evaluation framework, construction pipeline, and synthetic corpus to enable future research on context-aware speech synthesis

### [HumDial-EIBench: A Human-Recorded Multi-Turn Emotional Intelligence Benchmark for Audio Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11594)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces HumDial-EIBench, a benchmark for evaluating emotional intelligence in audio language models using real human-recorded dialogues, reformulating emotional tracking and causal reasoning tasks into multiple-choice questions to reduce subjective scoring bias. Unlike existing benchmarks that rely on synthesized speech and single-turn interactions, this approach uses authentic multi-turn conversations and includes novel acoustic-semantic conflict tasks to test model robustness against contradictory multimodal signals. Evaluation of eight ALMs revealed that current models struggle with multi-turn emotional reasoning and exhibit strong text-dominance bias, with decoupled processing of textual versus acoustic emotional cues.

**Key points:**

- Introduces HumDial-EIBench, the first emotional intelligence benchmark using real human-recorded dialogues instead of synthesized speech, addressing key limitations of existing benchmarks
- Reformulates emotional tracking and causal reasoning tasks into multiple-choice questions with adversarial distractors, reducing subjective scoring bias compared to open-ended evaluation methods
- Introduces novel acoustic-semantic conflict task to evaluate model robustness when audio and text convey contradictory emotional information
- Evaluation of eight audio language models reveals critical weaknesses: poor multi-turn emotional tracking, difficulty with implicit causal reasoning, and severe text-dominance bias during cross-modal conflicts
- Discovers that all tested models exhibit decoupled textual and acoustic empathy, suggesting fundamental limitations in multimodal emotional understanding

### [CoMelSinger: Discrete Token-Based Zero-Shot Singing Synthesis With Structured Melody Control and Guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19883)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

CoMelSinger presents a zero-shot singing voice synthesis system that uses discrete tokens and structured melody control, built on the non-autoregressive MaskGCT architecture with lyric and pitch tokens instead of conventional text inputs. The key innovation is addressing "prosody leakage" - where pitch information becomes entangled with timbre prompts - through a coarse-to-fine contrastive learning strategy that explicitly separates melody control from acoustic prompts, plus a lightweight Singing Voice Transcription module for frame-level supervision. The authors report improvements in pitch accuracy, timbre consistency, and zero-shot transferability, though the abstract doesn't identify specific limitations of the approach.

**Key points:**

- Introduces CoMelSinger, a zero-shot singing voice synthesis framework that addresses 'prosody leakage' - where pitch information becomes entangled in timbre prompts, compromising melody controllability in discrete codec-based approaches
- Proposes coarse-to-fine contrastive learning strategy to explicitly regularize pitch redundancy between acoustic prompts and melody inputs, enabling disentangled melody control
- Built on non-autoregressive MaskGCT architecture with lyric and pitch tokens replacing conventional text inputs, preserving in-context generalization while enhancing melody conditioning
- Incorporates lightweight encoder-only Singing Voice Transcription (SVT) module for frame-level supervision and acoustic-pitch-duration alignment
- Demonstrates notable improvements in pitch accuracy, timbre consistency, and zero-shot transferability compared to competitive baselines
