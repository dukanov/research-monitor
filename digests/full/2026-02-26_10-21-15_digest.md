### [FlexiCodec: A Dynamic Neural Audio Codec for Low Frame Rates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00981)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

FlexiCodec introduces a dynamic frame rate neural audio codec that achieves very low frame rates (3-12.5Hz) for speech language models by adaptively merging semantically similar frames in information-sparse regions, using ASR feature-assisted dual stream encoding with Transformer bottlenecks. Unlike existing 12.5Hz codecs, it provides inference-time controllable frame rates and better preserves semantic information at lower rates through its dynamic approach. The main challenge addressed is semantic information loss that occurs with very low frame rate tokenization.

**Key points:**

- FlexiCodec introduces a dynamic frame rate approach that adaptively merges semantically similar frames, allowing inference-time controllable frame rates between 3Hz and 12.5Hz
- Key challenge identified: very low frame rate neural audio codecs suffer from missing semantic information, which FlexiCodec addresses through novel architecture design
- Novel architecture features ASR feature-assisted dual stream encoding and Transformer bottlenecks to improve semantic preservation
- FlexiCodec demonstrates superior semantic information preservation compared to baseline systems while maintaining high audio reconstruction quality at extremely low frame rates (6.25Hz, 8.3Hz, 12.5Hz)
- Successfully validated for language model-based text-to-speech applications, showing practical utility beyond basic audio coding

### [The Design Space of Tri-Modal Masked Diffusion Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.21472)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This work introduces the first tri-modal masked diffusion model trained from scratch on text, image, and audio data, systematically analyzing scaling laws and training dynamics across modalities including a novel SDE-based batch size reparameterization. Unlike previous approaches that adapt unimodal models for bimodal tasks, they train a unified 3B-parameter model on 6.4T tokens demonstrating capabilities across text generation, text-to-image, and text-to-speech tasks. The authors acknowledge this as a "preliminary" model, suggesting the work represents an early but systematic exploration of large-scale multimodal discrete diffusion rather than a fully optimized system.

**Key points:**

- First tri-modal masked diffusion model pretrained from scratch on text, image-text, and audio-text data, diverging from previous fine-tuning approaches
- Novel SDE-based reparameterization that decouples physical batch size (compute constraints) from logical batch size (gradient variance optimization), eliminating need for batch size tuning
- Systematic analysis of multimodal scaling laws, modality mixing ratios, noise schedules, and batch-size effects with optimized inference sampling defaults
- Pretrained 3B-parameter model on 6.4T tokens achieving strong performance across text generation, text-to-image, and text-to-speech tasks
- Largest-scale systematic open study of multimodal discrete diffusion models conducted to date, providing insights into scaling behaviors across multiple modalities
