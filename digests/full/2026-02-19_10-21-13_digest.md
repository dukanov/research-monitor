### [Scaling Open Discrete Audio Foundation Models with Interleaved Semantic, Acoustic, and Text Tokens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16687)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents SODA, a family of native audio foundation models (135M to 4B parameters) that jointly model semantic content, acoustic details, and text using next-token prediction on discrete audio tokens. Unlike existing text-first audio language models or semantic-only approaches, SODA natively processes both semantic and acoustic information in a unified architecture, supported by the first scaling law study for discrete audio models showing optimal data grows 1.6× faster than model size. The approach demonstrates flexibility across diverse audio/text tasks, though it remains limited by the inherent quantization constraints of discrete token representations.

**Key points:**

- First systematic scaling law study for discrete audio models using IsoFLOP analysis across 64 models, finding optimal data grows 1.6× faster than optimal model size
- Novel native audio foundation models that jointly model semantic content, acoustic details, and text tokens through next-token prediction, moving beyond text-first approaches
- SODA model suite trained from 135M to 4B parameters on 500B tokens, demonstrating scalable training recipe with comprehensive design choice validation
- Unified architecture serves as flexible backbone for diverse audio/text tasks, demonstrated through voice-preserving speech-to-speech translation fine-tuning
- Systematic empirical investigation of key design factors including data sources, text mixture ratios, and token composition for audio foundation model training

### [Voice Impression Control in Zero-Shot TTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05688)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents a method for controlling voice impressions (e.g., dark-bright, rough-smooth characteristics) in zero-shot text-to-speech by using low-dimensional vectors to represent impression pair intensities. The key innovation is enabling impression control beyond speaker fidelity in zero-shot TTS, with the ability to generate target impressions from natural language descriptions via large language models rather than manual parameter tuning. The authors demonstrate effectiveness through both objective and subjective evaluations, though the abstract doesn't specify particular limitations of the approach.

**Key points:**

- Introduces a novel voice impression control method for zero-shot TTS using low-dimensional vectors to represent intensities of voice impression pairs (e.g., dark-bright)
- Addresses the challenging gap in zero-shot TTS: while speaker fidelity is high, controlling subtle para-/non-linguistic information for perceived voice characteristics remains difficult
- Enables natural language-driven impression control by generating the impression vector via large language models, eliminating manual optimization
- Demonstrates effectiveness through both objective and subjective evaluations in controlling voice impressions while maintaining zero-shot TTS capabilities
