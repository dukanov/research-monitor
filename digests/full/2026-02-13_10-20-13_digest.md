### [WavBench: Benchmarking Reasoning, Colloquialism, and Paralinguistics for End-to-End Spoken Dialogue Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12135)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

WavBench introduces a three-part benchmark for evaluating spoken dialogue models across reasoning capabilities (Pro subset), natural colloquial speech patterns prioritizing "listenability" over written accuracy (Basic subset), and comprehensive paralinguistic understanding and generation (Acoustic subset). Unlike existing evaluations that apply text-generation standards to spoken dialogue, WavBench specifically addresses audio-centric characteristics like natural vocabulary, linguistic fluency, and acoustic features that are crucial for real-world conversational AI. The benchmark was tested on five state-of-the-art models to provide insights into the intersection of complex reasoning, colloquial delivery, and paralinguistic fidelity.

**Key points:**

- WavBench introduces a novel tripartite evaluation framework with Pro (reasoning-enhanced), Basic (colloquialism prioritizing 'listenability'), and Acoustic (paralinguistic) subsets to address gaps in current spoken dialogue model evaluation
- Current evaluations predominantly follow text-generation standards and overlook unique audio-centric characteristics like paralinguistics and colloquialisms that are crucial for realistic conversational abilities
- The Basic subset establishes a new standard for spoken colloquialism that prioritizes 'listenability' through natural vocabulary, linguistic fluency, and interactive rapport rather than rigid written accuracy
- The benchmark addresses the urgent need for evaluations that transcend simple interactions and tackle real-world complexity as advanced reasoning capabilities are rapidly integrated into spoken dialogue models
- WavBench evaluates five state-of-the-art models to provide critical insights into the intersection of complex problem-solving, colloquial delivery, and paralinguistic fidelity

### [MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer: Scaling Audio Tokenizers for Future Audio Foundation Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.10934)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer, a 1.6B parameter discrete audio tokenizer built on a purely Transformer-based architecture (CAT - Causal Audio Tokenizer) that jointly optimizes encoder, quantizer, and decoder end-to-end on 3M hours of diverse audio data. Unlike existing approaches that rely on pretrained encoders, semantic distillation, or CNN architectures with fixed inductive biases, this homogeneous Transformer design scales gracefully and achieves superior reconstruction fidelity across speech, sound, and music domains. The resulting tokens enable competitive performance in downstream tasks like TTS and ASR, positioning this as a unified, scalable foundation for audio processing models.

**Key points:**

- CAT (Causal Audio Tokenizer with Transformer) introduces a purely Transformer-based architecture that learns discrete audio tokenization fully end-to-end, avoiding the fixed inductive biases of prior CNN-based approaches that rely on pretrained encoders or semantic distillation
- MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer scales to 1.6 billion parameters trained on 3 million hours of diverse audio data, demonstrating graceful scaling with predictable improvements and consistently outperforming prior codecs across speech, sound, and music domains
- The model enables the first purely autoregressive text-to-speech system that surpasses prior non-autoregressive and cascaded approaches, while also achieving competitive automatic speech recognition performance without auxiliary encoders
- The homogeneous Transformer-based design provides a unified, scalable interface that supports high-fidelity reconstruction across diverse audio domains, positioning it as a foundation for next-generation native audio models
