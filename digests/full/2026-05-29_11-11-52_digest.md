### [HoliTok:A Coutinuous Holistic Tokenization with Robust Dual Capabilities of Speech Generation and Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29948)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

HoliTok proposes a continuous speech tokenization approach that encodes 48 kHz speech into compact 25 Hz sequences of 128-dimensional latents, designed to enable unified speech generation and understanding in a single AR+DiT model architecture. Unlike existing discrete speech tokenizers, HoliTok uses a progressive training strategy to simultaneously maintain signal fidelity, semantic information, and language model learnability, claiming to be the only evaluated representation that works robustly in unified generation-understanding tasks without additional optimization tricks. The evaluation appears limited to the authors' specific unified architecture, which may not fully demonstrate generalizability across different model designs.

**Key points:**

- HoliTok introduces a continuous holistic tokenization that simultaneously satisfies language model learnability and high-quality waveform decodability, addressing a key limitation where existing speech tokenizers fail at both requirements
- The model encodes 48 kHz speech into compact 25 Hz sequences of 128-dimensional latents using a progressive training strategy that jointly preserves signal fidelity, semantic information, and latent learnability
- A unified AR+DiT architecture enables both speech synthesis and recognition using the same latent sequence, supporting generation-specific and unified generation-understanding tasks without architectural modifications
- HoliTok is the only evaluated representation that operates robustly in unified generation-understanding architectures without requiring additional optimization tricks, while achieving competitive reconstruction fidelity and improved generative learnability

### [MELD: Mel-Spectrogram-Based Speech Language Modeling with Discrete Latent Variables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29859)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

MELD introduces a discrete latent variable model that jointly optimizes both the encoder and speech language model on mel spectrograms, unlike existing approaches that train these components separately. This joint optimization approach outperforms codec-based and other mel-spectrogram baselines on zero-shot text-to-speech and speech-to-text tasks while reducing common autoregressive modeling issues like prolonged silence generation and word omissions. The key innovation is making the encoder aware of downstream objectives rather than optimizing it in isolation.

**Key points:**

- Introduces joint optimization of encoder and speech language model using discrete latent variables on mel spectrograms, addressing the limitation of separately optimized encoders being suboptimal for downstream tasks
- Achieves improvements over codec-based and mel-spectrogram baselines on zero-shot Text-to-Speech and Speech-to-Text tasks through joint optimization approach
- Effectively alleviates common autoregressive mel-spectrogram modeling issues including prolonged silence generation and word omissions
- Challenges the standard paradigm of separate encoder optimization in speech language models by demonstrating benefits of end-to-end joint training

### [Native Audio-Visual Alignment for Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.30073)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

NAVA proposes a two-stage approach for joint audio-video generation: first establishing audio-video correspondence in a dedicated interaction space, then applying external context conditioning during joint denoising using an Align-then-Fuse MMDiT architecture. This differs from existing methods that either use dual-tower designs with weaker audio-video co-evolution or fully unified tri-modal approaches that mix semantic conditioning with low-level synchronization. The method achieves competitive results with only 6.3B parameters and includes controllable speech timbre through Timbre-in-Context Conditioning.

**Key points:**

- NAVA introduces a novel Native Audio-Visual Alignment framework that first establishes audio-video correspondence in a dedicated interaction space before applying external context conditioning, addressing limitations of existing dual-tower and tri-modal approaches
- The Align-then-Fuse MMDiT architecture transitions from modality-aware audio-video alignment to modality-shared joint denoising, enabling better fine-grained audio-video co-evolution compared to posterior alignment methods
- Timbre-in-Context Conditioning technique allows controllable speech timbre by associating reference timbre cues with corresponding speech spans
- Achieves superior performance in video quality, audio-visual synchronization, and reference-timbre controllability using only 6.3B parameters, demonstrating efficiency compared to larger models
- Addresses key limitation where existing methods either weaken fine-grained audio-video co-evolution (dual-tower) or inappropriately couple semantic conditioning with low-level synchronization (tri-modal)
