### [ATRIE: Adaptive Tuning for Robust Inference and Emotion in Persona-Driven Speech Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19055)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

ATRIE introduces a dual-track architecture (P2-DT) for character voice synthesis that separates voice generation into a static Timbre Track (using scalar quantization for identity preservation) and a dynamic Prosody Track (using hierarchical flow-matching for emotional expression), with knowledge distilled from a 14B language model. This disentanglement approach enables maintaining consistent persona traits across different emotional contexts, achieving strong performance in both speaker verification (EER: 0.04) and cross-modal retrieval (mAP: 0.75) on anime character voices. The method addresses a key limitation of existing systems that struggle to preserve character identity while expressing diverse emotions.

**Key points:**

- Introduces ATRIE framework with novel Persona-Prosody Dual-Track (P2-DT) architecture that disentangles voice generation into static Timbre Track (Scalar Quantization) and dynamic Prosody Track (Hierarchical Flow-Matching)
- Addresses key limitation of existing systems by maintaining consistent persona traits across diverse emotional contexts in character voice synthesis
- Leverages knowledge distillation from a 14B LLM teacher to enhance speech synthesis capabilities for persona-driven applications
- Achieves strong performance metrics with Zero-Shot Speaker Verification EER of 0.04 and cross-modal retrieval mAP of 0.75 on extended AnimeTTS-Bench dataset
- Targets specific application domain of anime avatars and digital humans for immersive multimedia content creation

### [Affectron: Emotional Speech Synthesis with Affective and Contextually Aligned Nonverbal Vocalizations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14432)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Affectron addresses the challenge of generating contextually appropriate nonverbal vocalizations (laughter, sighs, etc.) in emotional speech synthesis by introducing an NV-augmented training strategy that expands the diversity of nonverbal vocalization types and insertion locations, combined with structural masking applied to a speech model pre-trained on verbal-only speech. The approach differs from existing work by explicitly tackling the limited supervision problem for nonverbal vocalizations through data augmentation and by integrating NV generation into a pre-trained verbal speech backbone. The method is constrained by working with small-scale datasets, though the augmentation strategy aims to mitigate the limited NV data availability.

**Key points:**

- Affectron framework introduces NV-augmented training strategy to expand distribution of nonverbal vocalization types and insertion locations despite limited NV data
- Novel approach uses NV structural masking with speech backbone pre-trained on purely verbal speech to enable diverse and natural nonverbal vocalization synthesis
- Key challenge addressed: learning diverse and contextually aligned nonverbal vocalizations (laughter, sighs) in open settings with limited data and lack of explicit supervision
- Experimental results show Affectron produces more expressive and diverse nonverbal vocalizations than baseline systems while preserving naturalness of verbal speech stream
- Framework built on small-scale open and decoupled corpus, suggesting efficient approach for emotional speech synthesis with limited resources

### [Computational Narrative Understanding for Expressive Text-to-Speech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04072)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces LibriQuote, a 5.3K hour dataset of expressive speech extracted from character dialogue in audiobooks, with contextual pseudo-labels describing intended delivery style (e.g., "whispered softly"). Unlike previous work using general speech corpora, they specifically leverage the natural expressiveness found in fictional character quotations, showing that fine-tuning flow-matching models and training autoregressive TTS models on this data improves both expressivity and intelligibility. The work provides a new resource for training more expressive TTS systems, though the abstract doesn't highlight specific technical limitations.

**Key points:**

- Introduced LibriQuote, a novel 5.3K hour dataset of expressive speech extracted from character quotations in audiobooks, supplemented with contextual pseudo-labels for speech verbs and adverbs
- Leveraged the natural alternation between neutral narration and expressive character dialogue in fictional audiobooks as a rich source of prosodic diversity for TTS training
- Fine-tuning flow-matching models on LibriQuote yielded substantial improvements in both expressivity and intelligibility compared to existing approaches
- Benchmarking revealed significant variability across TTS systems in their ability to generate expressive speech, highlighting gaps in current model capabilities
