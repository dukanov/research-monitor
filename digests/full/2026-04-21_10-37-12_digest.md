### [MoVE: Translating Laughter and Tears via Mixture of Vocalization Experts in Speech-to-Speech Translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17435)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

MoVE addresses a key limitation in speech-to-speech translation where existing systems strip away non-verbal vocalizations like laughter and crying, proposing a Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts architecture that uses specialized adapters and soft-weighting to preserve these expressive elements. The approach differs from existing work by successfully reproducing target non-verbal vocalizations in 76% of cases compared to at most 14% for current systems, while requiring only 30 minutes of curated data when leveraging pretrained AudioLLMs. The evaluation appears limited to English-Chinese translation, so generalization to other language pairs remains to be demonstrated.

**Key points:**

- Addresses critical limitation in S2ST systems that strip away non-verbal vocalizations (laughter, crying) which convey pragmatic intent and limit real-world utility
- Introduces MoVE: Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts architecture with expressive-specialized adapters and soft-weighting router to capture hybrid expressive states
- Proposes synthesis pipeline for building scalable expressive datasets to overcome data scarcity in emotional speech translation
- Demonstrates striking data efficiency: only 30 minutes of curated data needed for strong performance using pretrained AudioLLMs
- Achieves 76% reproduction of target non-verbal vocalizations vs maximum 14% for existing S2ST systems, with highest human-rated naturalness and emotional fidelity

### [LLM-Codec: Neural Audio Codec Meets Language Model Objectives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17852)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

LLM-Codec addresses the mismatch between neural audio codecs (designed for waveform reconstruction) and their use as tokenizers in spoken language models by augmenting codec training with language model objectives. The approach adds future token prediction with multi-step heads and semantic alignment between audio and text representations via contrastive learning, using a differentiable Gumbel bridge for end-to-end training. Results show significant improvements in speech coherence (+12.1 points) and 35% perplexity reduction while maintaining reconstruction fidelity, demonstrating that optimization for both audio quality and token predictability can be achieved simultaneously.

**Key points:**

- LLM-Codec addresses the mismatch between neural audio codecs optimized for waveform reconstruction and their use as tokenizers for autoregressive language models, which injects uncertainty and increases perplexity
- The approach augments codec training with language-model-facing objectives using two key innovations: future token prediction with Medusa-style multi-step heads and semantic alignment via memory-bank contrastive loss
- Uses a differentiable Gumbel bridge to enable end-to-end gradients from language model objectives to the codec encoder while keeping both codec and LLM architectures unchanged
- Achieves significant improvements on SALMon speech coherence (61.6% accuracy, +12.1 points) while reducing perplexity by 35, and improves speech Mel distance by 5.0% on Codec-SUPERB-tiny
- Demonstrates that reconstruction fidelity and token predictability can be improved simultaneously, challenging the assumed trade-off between these objectives

### [MINT-Bench: A Comprehensive Multilingual Benchmark for Instruction-Following Text-to-Speech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17958)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

MINT-Bench introduces a comprehensive multilingual benchmark for evaluating instruction-following text-to-speech systems across 10 languages, using a hierarchical taxonomy and hybrid evaluation protocol that assesses content consistency, instruction adherence, and perceptual quality. Unlike existing evaluations with limited coverage and weak diagnostic granularity, this benchmark provides detailed multilingual assessment capabilities and reveals that even leading commercial systems struggle significantly with compositional and paralinguistic control tasks. The evaluation shows current TTS systems remain far from solved, with major bottlenecks in complex instruction-following scenarios.

**Key points:**

- MINT-Bench introduces a comprehensive multilingual benchmark for instruction-following TTS built on hierarchical multi-axis taxonomy and scalable multi-stage data construction pipeline
- The benchmark employs a novel hierarchical hybrid evaluation protocol that jointly assesses content consistency, instruction following, and perceptual quality across ten languages
- Experiments reveal that current TTS systems remain far from solved, with commercial systems leading overall but open-source models achieving competitive performance and even outperforming commercial systems in localized settings like Chinese
- Compositional and paralinguistic controls are identified as major bottlenecks for current instruction-following TTS systems
- The work addresses critical gaps in prior evaluation approaches including limited benchmark coverage, weak diagnostic granularity, and insufficient multilingual support

### [Mechanisms of Multimodal Synchronization: Insights from Decoder-Based Video-Text-to-Speech Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17690)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper investigates how unified decoder-only transformers synchronize modalities with different temporal sampling rates, using video-text-to-speech synthesis as a controlled testbed with their Visatronic model trained on VoxCeleb2. The work provides mechanistic insights into positional encoding strategies (finding both global sequential and co-temporal ordered indexing work well), modality ordering trade-offs (video-first for in-domain performance vs. text-first for cross-domain transfer), and introduces TimeSync, a phoneme-level synchronization metric for fine-grained temporal analysis. The approach differs from existing work by systematically analyzing the specific mechanisms enabling multimodal synchronization rather than just demonstrating synthesis capabilities.

**Key points:**

- Introduces two effective positional encoding strategies for multimodal synchronization: 'global sequential indexing' and 'co-temporal ordered indexing', with the latter providing a simpler mechanism without requiring explicit timestamp metadata
- Reveals that text and video contribute complementary information in speech synthesis - text ensures intelligibility while video provides temporal cues and emotional expressiveness
- Discovers a consistent trade-off in modality ordering: video-first ordering achieves stronger in-domain performance while text-first ordering generalizes more robustly to unseen domains
- Proposes TimeSync, a novel phoneme-level metric that reveals temporal misalignments overlooked by traditional frame-level metrics
- Establishes video-text-to-speech synthesis as a valuable controlled testbed for understanding temporal synchronization mechanisms in unified multimodal decoder transformers

### [Anonymization, Not Elimination: Utility-Preserved Speech Anonymization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17000)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents a two-stage speech anonymization framework that protects both linguistic content (by replacing personally identifiable information through generative speech editing) and acoustic identity (via F3-VA, a flow-matching-based method that generates diverse anonymized speakers) while preserving data utility for downstream tasks. Unlike existing methods that often degrade acoustic continuity and vocal diversity, this approach maintains usability for ASR, TTS, and speech emotion recognition, as demonstrated through training models from scratch rather than just testing with pretrained models. The work also introduces more comprehensive privacy evaluation using both acoustic and content-based speaker verification metrics.

**Key points:**

- Proposes F3-VA, a novel flow-matching-based anonymization framework with three-stage design that produces diverse and distinct anonymized speakers while preserving acoustic continuity
- Introduces two-stage approach protecting both linguistic content (via generative speech editing for PII replacement) and acoustic identity, addressing limitations of existing methods that degrade data utility
- Develops comprehensive evaluation protocol that trains ASR/TTS/SER models from scratch on anonymized data, providing more realistic utility assessment compared to standard pretrained model testing
- Achieves stronger privacy protection with minimal utility degradation compared to VoicePrivacy Challenge baselines, demonstrating feasibility of utility-preserved anonymization
- Addresses critical gap where existing anonymization methods disrupt acoustic continuity and reduce vocal diversity, compromising downstream task performance

### [Prosody as Supervision: Bridging the Non-Verbal--Verbal for Multilingual Speech Emotion Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17647)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces NOVA-ARC, a framework that uses non-verbal vocalizations (emotional sounds without words) as supervision to train multilingual speech emotion recognition models for verbal speech, rather than relying on labeled verbal speech data. The key innovation is reformulating the problem as "non-verbal-to-verbal transfer," leveraging the hypothesis that prosodic emotion cues in non-verbal sounds may transfer more effectively across languages than verbal speech patterns. The approach uses hyperbolic geometry to model emotional structures and optimal transport to align emotion prototypes between non-verbal source data and unlabeled verbal target speech across multiple languages.

**Key points:**

- First work to introduce non-verbal-to-verbal transfer paradigm for speech emotion recognition, moving beyond verbal-speech-centric supervision by leveraging non-verbal vocalizations as supervision for multilingual emotion recognition
- Proposes NOVA-ARC framework that models affective structure in hyperbolic Poincaré ball geometry with vector-quantized prosody codebook and hyperbolic emotion lens to capture emotion intensity
- Reformulates low-resource multilingual SER problem as non-verbal-to-verbal transfer rather than conventional verbal-to-verbal approaches, addressing poor cross-lingual transfer issues
- Uses optimal transport based prototype alignment between source emotion prototypes and target utterances for unsupervised adaptation with consistency regularization
- Demonstrates superior performance over Euclidean counterparts and strong self-supervised learning baselines in both adaptation settings

### [FreezeEmpath: Efficient Training for Empathetic Spoken Chatbots with Frozen LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18159)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

FreezeEmpath presents an efficient training approach for empathetic spoken chatbots that keeps the underlying large language model frozen while training only additional components using existing speech instruction data and speech emotion recognition datasets. This method avoids the need for expensive empathetic speech instruction data and prevents catastrophic forgetting that can occur when fine-tuning LLMs with cross-modal empathetic data. The authors report improved emotional expressiveness in generated speech and better performance on empathetic dialogue, speech emotion recognition, and spoken question-answering tasks compared to other empathetic models.

**Key points:**

- FreezeEmpath introduces a novel training approach that keeps LLM parameters frozen to prevent catastrophic forgetting and degradation of general capabilities during empathetic chatbot training
- The method eliminates the need for costly empathetic speech instruction data by relying solely on existing speech instruction data and speech emotion recognition (SER) data
- Unlike prior work that finetunes LLMs with cross-modal empathetic instruction data, FreezeEmpath maintains the base LLM's capabilities while adding empathetic speech generation abilities
- Experimental results show the model generates emotionally expressive speech and outperforms other empathetic models across multiple tasks including empathetic dialogue, SER, and SpokenQA
- The approach addresses two key challenges in empathetic spoken dialogue systems: high training data costs and lack of emotional expressiveness in generated speech

### [Hierarchical Codec Diffusion for Video-to-Speech Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.15923)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents HiCoDiT, a hierarchical codec diffusion transformer for video-to-speech generation that leverages the multi-level structure of RVQ-based speech codecs, where low-level tokens capture speaker semantics and high-level tokens encode prosodic details. Unlike existing methods that ignore speech hierarchy, HiCoDiT uses separate processing blocks - low-level blocks conditioned on lip motion and facial identity for speaker-aware content, and high-level blocks using facial expressions for prosodic modulation. The approach shows improved fidelity and expressiveness compared to baselines, though like all video-to-speech methods, it remains limited by the inherent challenge of inferring complete acoustic information from visual cues alone.

**Key points:**

- HiCoDiT introduces a novel Hierarchical Codec Diffusion Transformer that exploits the hierarchical structure of RVQ-based speech codecs, where lower-level tokens encode coarse speaker semantics and higher-level tokens capture fine-grained prosody
- The method uses specialized conditioning at different hierarchical levels: low-level blocks condition on lip-synchronized motion and facial identity for speaker-aware content, while high-level blocks use facial expression to modulate prosodic dynamics
- A dual-scale adaptive instance layer normalization is proposed that jointly captures global vocal style through channel-wise normalization and local prosody dynamics through temporal-wise normalization
- Unlike existing VTS methods that disregard speech hierarchy, this approach achieves stronger audio-visual alignment by matching visual features to specific hierarchical speech levels
- Experimental results show HiCoDiT outperforms baselines in both fidelity and expressiveness, demonstrating the potential of discrete modeling for video-to-speech generation
