### [Can We Hear from Events? Generating Speech from Event Camera](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26672)

**Relevance:** 85.0%

This paper introduces EventSpeech, a framework that generates speech from text using event cameras (neuromorphic sensors) that capture microsecond-precise illumination changes rather than traditional RGB frames. The key innovation addresses temporal granularity mismatch in conventional approaches, where fixed camera exposure times blur high-frequency articulatory details essential for emotional speech, whereas event cameras naturally align with acoustic waveform dynamics. The approach requires specialized neuromorphic hardware and introduces a new benchmark dataset (EVT-SPK), though practical adoption may be limited by the availability of such specialized sensors.

**Key points:**

- Introduces EventSpeech, the first framework to use neuromorphic event cameras for speech generation, addressing temporal granularity mismatch in traditional RGB-based approaches where fixed exposure times blur high-frequency articulatory movements
- Event cameras' microsecond-precise temporal resolution naturally aligns with acoustic waveform dynamics, enabling better preservation of fine-grained emotions in generated speech
- Proposes novel architecture combining Event Encoder for sparse neuromorphic data with Hierarchical Wavelet Contextualizer and bidirectional alignment mechanism for synchronizing linguistic, visual, and acoustic features
- Creates EVT-SPK, the first large-scale benchmark dataset combining synthetic data and real-world neuromorphic hardware recordings for event-based speech generation research
- Demonstrates significant performance improvements over existing baselines in emotional speech quality and motion blur resistance, establishing new paradigm for multimodal speech synthesis

### [PilotTTS: A Disciplined Modular Recipe for Competitive Speech Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27258)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

PilotTTS presents a lightweight autoregressive TTS system that achieves competitive performance using 200K hours of training data with a minimalist architecture employing Q-Former-based conditioning to decouple speaker identity from speaking style. The key differentiator is its unified framework supporting multiple synthesis tasks (zero-shot voice cloning, emotion synthesis, paralinguistic synthesis, and Chinese dialects) while using only open-source data processing tools and cross-sample paired training. The system achieves strong results on Seed-TTS Eval benchmark with 1.50% WER on English and high speaker similarity scores, though the claim of using "minimal" data is questionable given that 200K hours represents a substantial dataset.

**Key points:**

- PilotTTS achieves competitive TTS performance using only 200K hours of data (vs typical millions) through minimalist architecture and rigorous data engineering with open-source tools
- Novel Q-Former-based conditioning approach decouples speaker identity from speaking style via cross-sample paired training within a unified framework
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on Seed-TTS Eval with lowest WER (1.50% test-en, 0.87% CER test-zh) and highest speaker similarity scores, outperforming larger systems
- Unified framework supports multiple synthesis tasks: zero-shot voice cloning, emotion synthesis (11 categories), paralinguistic synthesis (4 categories), and Chinese dialect synthesis (14 dialects)
- Provides complete reproducible pipeline addressing resource constraints for research teams, with full code and pretrained weights release

### [CosyEdit2: Speech-Editing-Oriented Reinforcement Learning Unlocks Better Zero-Shot TTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25930)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

CosyEdit2 introduces a two-stage training framework for speech editing that combines supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning (Group Relative Policy Optimization) on target-speech-free data, addressing limitations of purely supervised approaches. Unlike prior work that relied solely on supervised fine-tuning with imperfect paired data, this method uses RL to optimize editing performance without requiring target speech during the second training stage. The authors demonstrate that better speech editing capability also improves zero-shot TTS performance, suggesting these tasks share beneficial underlying representations.

**Key points:**

- Introduces a novel two-stage post-training framework progressing from supervised editing initialization to editing-oriented Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) over target-speech-free data
- Addresses key limitations of prior Supervised Fine-Tuning approaches which were bottlenecked by imperfect paired editing data and coarse-grained optimization signals
- Demonstrates that improving speech editing performance simultaneously unlocks better zero-shot TTS capability, revealing a deeper mutual relationship between these tasks
- Speech editing requires far stricter local acoustic consistency with surrounding unedited content compared to zero-shot TTS, despite sharing similar generative foundations
