### [EmoTransCap: Dataset and Pipeline for Emotion Transition-Aware Speech Captioning in Discourses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26417)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

This paper introduces EmoTransCap, a speech captioning system that captures dynamic emotion transitions across entire discourses rather than just static emotions in isolated sentences. The key innovation is creating the first large-scale dataset and automated pipeline specifically designed to model how emotions change over time in speech, using a Multi-Task Emotion Transition Recognition model that jointly detects transitions and performs speaker diarization. The work extends beyond existing single-emotion speech captioning by incorporating temporal dynamics and discourse-level context, enabling more realistic emotional speech synthesis for conversational agents.

**Key points:**

- First large-scale dataset explicitly designed to capture discourse-level emotion transitions in speech, addressing limitations of existing systems that only handle static, single-emotion characterization within isolated sentences
- Novel EmoTransCap paradigm integrates temporal emotion dynamics with discourse-level speech description, moving beyond current speech emotion captioning (SEC) systems
- Multi-Task Emotion Transition Recognition (MTETR) model performs joint emotion transition detection and diarization, incorporating acoustic attributes and temporal cues from discourse-level speech
- Automated pipeline for dataset creation enables scalable expansion while maintaining rich emotion transition content, with both descriptive and instruction-oriented annotation versions using LLMs
- Introduces controllable, transition-aware emotional speech synthesis system at discourse level to enhance anthropomorphic emotional expressiveness in conversational agents

### [The False Resonance: A Critical Examination of Emotion Embedding Similarity for Speech Generation Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26347)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper critically examines the widely-used practice of evaluating emotional speech generation by computing cosine similarity between emotion embeddings (e.g., emotion2vec) of reference and generated samples. Through adversarial testing and human alignment studies, the authors demonstrate that these embeddings are unsuitable for zero-shot similarity evaluation because linguistic and speaker variations interfere with emotional features, causing the metric to reward acoustic mimicry rather than genuine emotional synthesis. The key finding is that despite high classification accuracy, these emotion similarity metrics misalign with human perception of emotional expressiveness in generated speech.

**Key points:**

- Challenges the widely-used assumption that emotion embeddings (e.g., emotion2vec) are suitable for zero-shot similarity evaluation in speech generation, despite their high classification accuracy
- Demonstrates through controlled adversarial tasks that linguistic and speaker variations interfere with emotional features in embedding spaces, overshadowing the target emotional information
- Shows that current emotion similarity metrics misalign with human perception and reward acoustic mimicry rather than genuine emotional synthesis
- Reveals a fundamental representational limitation where emotion encoders' discriminative ability degrades when used for similarity-based evaluation across different speakers and linguistic content
