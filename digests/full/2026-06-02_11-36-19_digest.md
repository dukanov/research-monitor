### [DUET: Unified Dual-Space Emotion Control for Diffusion and Flow-Matching Driven Text-to-Speech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00066)

**Relevance:** 80.0%

**DUET** introduces a plug-and-play framework for adding emotion control to existing pretrained diffusion and flow-matching TTS models by exploiting the discovery that emotion embeddings form a linearly decodable direction nearly orthogonal to speaker identity in the model's hidden states. The approach uses unified dual-space control—hidden space steering to guide generation along target emotion directions and mel-space guidance to refine spectral details via gradients from a differentiable vocoder—enabling fine-grained emotion control without retraining the base models. The method demonstrates superior performance over supervised emotional TTS baselines across five different TTS architectures and three datasets, though it requires a differentiable vocoder for the mel-space guidance component.

**Key points:**

- Emotion embeddings emerge as linearly decodable directions in frozen hidden states that are nearly orthogonal to speaker identity directions, enabling disentangled emotion control
- DUET provides plug-and-play emotion control for pretrained diffusion and flow-matching TTS models without requiring retraining or architectural modifications
- Unified dual-space control combines hidden space steering along emotion directions with mel-space guidance through differentiable vocoder gradients for fine-grained emotion intervention
- Outperforms 10 supervised state-of-the-art emotional TTS baselines across five diverse architectures and achieves highest human-rated emotion appropriateness scores

### [Sympatheia: Emotionally Adaptive Voice Assistant with Continuous Affect Conditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00851)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Sympatheia presents a speech-to-speech dialogue system that generates emotionally appropriate responses by conditioning on continuous valence-arousal (VA) signals inferred from user speech and supplementary multimodal inputs (facial expressions, biosignals, text). The key innovation is using continuous affect conditioning rather than discrete emotions, and integrating multiple sensing modalities to handle cases where speech alone provides weak emotional cues. The authors created Sympatheia-18k, a synthetic dialogue corpus with both emotional and neutral splits, and demonstrated improved semantic and vocal emotional appropriateness compared to baseline conversational systems.

**Key points:**

- Introduced Sympatheia, a speech-to-speech dialogue framework using continuous valence-arousal (VA) control signals for affect conditioning, addressing the challenge of weak or ambiguous emotional cues in everyday speech
- Created Sympatheia-18k dataset with 12 emotion anchors and dual splits: emotional split for learning affective behavior and neutral split pairing neutral queries with emotion-conditioned responses to isolate explicit emotion control
- Demonstrated that the same VA interface can integrate emotion estimates from diverse sensing modalities (facial expression, biosignals, textual descriptions) to improve response alignment when speech alone provides limited emotional evidence
- Empirical results show Sympatheia outperforms speech conversational baselines in generating responses with both semantically and prosodically appropriate emotional content
- Established continuous affect conditioning as an effective practical approach for building emotionally adaptive voice assistants that can respond appropriately across emotionally ambiguous conversational contexts

### [UniVocal: Unified Speech-Singing Code-Switching Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01677)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

UniVocal introduces a unified framework for Speech-Singing Code-Switching (SCS) synthesis that automatically transitions between speech and singing modes based solely on text context, without requiring explicit control tags. Unlike existing single-mode systems or those needing switching annotations, it uses curriculum learning and introduces refined cent tokens with Chain-of-Thought prosody planning to implicitly infer vocal modes from semantics. The work addresses data scarcity through synthetic data generation and introduces SCSBench benchmark, though limitations in semantic tokenizers capturing acoustic details required additional refinements.

**Key points:**

- UniVocal introduces Speech-Singing Code-Switching (SCS) Synthesis, where transitions between speech and singing are autonomously driven by textual semantics without explicit control tags
- The system employs a data-efficient two-stage curriculum learning strategy to progressively train a competitive TTS system for code-switching capabilities
- A scalable pipeline was developed to synthesize diverse code-switching data that is both semantically and acoustically natural, along with SCSBench benchmark for evaluation
- Novel technical contributions include refined cent tokens and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation for prosody planning to enhance empathetic speech and singing melody
- UniVocal achieves state-of-the-art performance on SCSBench while maintaining competitive performance on regular speech and singing tasks

### [Local Diagnostics of Continuous Normalizing Flow for Out-of-Distribution Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00684)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper proposes using continuous normalizing flows (CNFs) for out-of-distribution detection by developing a Lagrangian sub-flow framework that isolates relevant representation components while using others as context. The key innovation is addressing the "likelihood paradox" where CNFs assign high likelihood to out-of-distribution samples by replacing likelihood-based methods with geometric diagnostic signals derived from the velocity field over sub-flow trajectories. The authors demonstrate superior performance on zero-shot phoneme-level mispronunciation detection compared to traditional likelihood-based approaches, though the evaluation appears limited to speech synthesis applications.

**Key points:**

- Introduces Lagrangian sub-flow (LSF) framework for CNFs that isolates density estimation for relevant components while using remaining components as context for OOD detection
- Demonstrates that CNFs suffer from the 'likelihood paradox' where OOD samples receive erroneously high likelihood scores due to inductive bias prioritizing low-level details over high-level semantics
- Proposes novel geometric diagnostic signals based on velocity field dynamics over sub-flow trajectories as alternative to traditional likelihood-based OOD detection
- Shows superior performance on zero-shot phoneme-level mispronunciation detection compared to likelihood-based methods on real-world speech synthesis benchmarks
