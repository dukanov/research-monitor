### [DashengTokenizer: One layer is enough for unified audio understanding and generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23765)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

**Summary:**

DashengTokenizer presents a continuous audio tokenizer that inverts the conventional training paradigm by starting with frozen semantic features and injecting acoustic information, rather than training acoustic tokenizers first and then adding semantic knowledge. This approach achieves superior performance across 22 understanding tasks (speech emotion recognition, music understanding, acoustic scene classification) while maintaining competitive audio reconstruction quality, and outperforms VAE-based methods on text-to-audio and text-to-music generation tasks. The work challenges the assumption that VAE architectures are necessary for audio synthesis, demonstrating that a single-layer approach can effectively unify audio understanding and generation capabilities.

**Key points:**

- Inverts conventional paradigm by leveraging frozen semantic features and injecting acoustic information, rather than training acoustic tokenizers then integrating semantic knowledge
- Outperforms previous audio codec and encoder baselines across 22 diverse tasks while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality, demonstrating effectiveness for both understanding and generation
- Shows acoustic injection improves performance on speech emotion recognition, music understanding, and acoustic scene classification tasks
- Challenges the assumption that VAE-based architectures are prerequisites for audio synthesis, surpassing standard VAE methods on text-to-audio and text-to-music tasks
- Demonstrates that a single layer architecture can achieve unified audio understanding and generation capabilities

### [Human or Machine? A Preliminary Turing Test for Speech-to-Speech Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24080)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper presents the first systematic Turing test evaluation for speech-to-speech (S2S) conversational systems, collecting 2,968 human judgments across 9 state-of-the-art systems and finding that none pass the human-likeness test. Unlike previous text-based evaluations, they developed a fine-grained taxonomy of 18 human-likeness dimensions and found that failures stem primarily from paralinguistic features, emotional expressivity, and conversational persona rather than semantic understanding. The study reveals that off-the-shelf AI models are unreliable as Turing test judges, though the authors propose an interpretable alternative model for automatic evaluation.

**Key points:**

- First Turing test conducted specifically for speech-to-speech (S2S) systems, collecting 2,968 human judgments across 9 state-of-the-art systems - no existing S2S system passed the test
- The bottleneck for human-likeness is not semantic understanding but rather paralinguistic features, emotional expressivity, and conversational persona
- Developed a fine-grained taxonomy of 18 human-likeness dimensions for detailed diagnostic evaluation beyond binary pass/fail outcomes
- Off-the-shelf AI models perform unreliably as Turing test judges, highlighting limitations in automated evaluation approaches
- Proposed an interpretable model leveraging fine-grained human-likeness ratings to enable accurate and transparent human-vs-machine discrimination
