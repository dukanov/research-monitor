### [Emotion-Aligned Generation in Diffusion Text to Speech Models via Preference-Guided Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25416)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

This paper presents EASPO (Emotion-Aware Stepwise Preference Optimization), a post-training framework that improves emotional text-to-speech by optimizing diffusion models at intermediate denoising steps rather than only at the final output level. The key innovation is EASPM, a time-conditioned preference model that scores noisy intermediate speech states and enables automatic construction of preference pairs for fine-grained emotional control during generation. This differs from existing methods that rely on coarse emotional labels or proxy classifiers and only receive utterance-level feedback, with experiments showing improvements in both expressiveness and naturalness.

**Key points:**

- Introduces EASPO (Emotion-Aware Stepwise Preference Optimization), a novel post-training framework that optimizes emotional alignment at intermediate denoising steps rather than only at final output level
- Key innovation over prior work: moves from coarse emotion labels and utterance-level feedback to fine-grained emotional preferences applied during the diffusion process itself
- Develops EASPM, a time-conditioned model that can score noisy intermediate speech states and automatically construct preference pairs for training
- Achieves superior performance in both emotional expressiveness and speech naturalness compared to existing emotional TTS methods
- Enables controllable emotional shaping by optimizing generation to match stepwise preferences throughout the denoising process

### [Fed-PISA: Federated Voice Cloning via Personalized Identity-Style Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16010)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Fed-PISA proposes a federated learning approach for voice cloning that uses disentangled Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA): speaker identity features (ID-LoRA) remain local while only lightweight style features (style-LoRA) are shared with the server to reduce communication costs. The method differs from existing federated voice cloning by preserving stylistic diversity through a collaborative filtering-inspired aggregation that creates personalized models for each client based on stylistically similar peers, rather than suppressing heterogeneity. The authors report improvements in style expressivity, naturalness, and speaker similarity compared to standard federated baselines.

**Key points:**

- Fed-PISA introduces a novel disentangled Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) mechanism that keeps speaker timbre locally (private ID-LoRA) while only transmitting lightweight style parameters (style-LoRA) to minimize communication costs
- The approach uses collaborative filtering-inspired aggregation to create personalized models for each client by learning from stylistically similar peers, effectively harnessing heterogeneity rather than suppressing it
- Fed-PISA addresses key limitations in federated voice cloning: high communication costs and insufficient personalization due to stylistic heterogeneity suppression in existing approaches
- Experimental results demonstrate improvements in style expressivity, naturalness, and speaker similarity while maintaining minimal communication overhead compared to standard federated baselines
