### [Hierarchical Codec Diffusion for Video-to-Speech Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15923)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces HiCoDiT, a method for generating speech from silent video that explicitly models speech hierarchy using discrete tokens from Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) codecs, where lower-level tokens capture speaker identity and coarse semantics while higher-level tokens encode fine-grained prosody. Unlike existing video-to-speech methods that ignore speech's hierarchical structure, HiCoDiT uses separate processing blocks for different token levels—conditioning low-level blocks on lip motion and facial identity, and high-level blocks on facial expressions—along with a dual-scale normalization technique for better coarse-to-fine modeling. The approach demonstrates improved fidelity and expressiveness compared to baselines, though it relies on the quality of visual feature extraction and codec representations.

**Key points:**

- HiCoDiT introduces a novel Hierarchical Codec Diffusion Transformer that exploits the inherent hierarchy of discrete speech tokens, using low-level blocks for speaker-aware content (lip motion + facial identity) and high-level blocks for prosodic dynamics (facial expression)
- Addresses a key limitation in existing VTS methods that disregard the hierarchical nature of speech spanning from coarse speaker-aware semantics to fine-grained prosodic details, hindering proper audio-visual alignment
- Proposes dual-scale adaptive instance layer normalization that jointly captures global vocal style through channel-wise normalization and local prosody dynamics through temporal-wise normalization for effective coarse-to-fine conditioning
- Leverages RVQ-based codec structure where lower-level tokens encode coarse speaker semantics while higher-level tokens capture fine-grained prosody, enabling targeted conditioning at specific hierarchical levels
- Demonstrates superior performance over baselines in both fidelity and expressiveness, highlighting the potential of discrete modeling approaches for video-to-speech generation

### [NVBench: A Benchmark for Speech Synthesis with Non-Verbal Vocalizations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16211)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

**NVBench** introduces a standardized bilingual benchmark for evaluating text-to-speech systems' ability to generate non-verbal vocalizations (NVVs) like laughs, sighs, and sobs, featuring a 45-type taxonomy and multi-axis evaluation protocol that separately assesses speech quality from NVV-specific controllability, placement, and salience. Unlike existing limited evaluation approaches, this work provides a unified framework for fair cross-system comparison of diverse TTS control interfaces. Evaluation of 15 TTS systems reveals that NVV controllability often decouples from speech quality, with persistent challenges in low-SNR oral cues and long-duration affective vocalizations.

**Key points:**

- Introduces NVBench, the first standardized benchmark for evaluating speech synthesis systems' ability to generate non-verbal vocalizations (NVVs) like laughs, sighs, and sobs with proper controllability, placement, and salience
- Proposes a unified 45-type NVV taxonomy and multi-axis evaluation protocol that separates general speech naturalness from NVV-specific performance metrics
- Key finding that NVV controllability often decouples from speech quality - systems can excel in one dimension while failing in the other
- Identifies persistent bottlenecks across 15 benchmarked TTS systems: low-SNR oral cues and long-duration affective NVVs remain challenging to synthesize effectively
- Provides bilingual evaluation framework (English/Chinese) enabling fair cross-system comparison across diverse control interfaces under standardized conditions

### [Qwen3.5-Omni Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15804)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Qwen3.5-Omni is a large-scale multimodal model with hundreds of billions of parameters that processes text, vision, and audio through a Hybrid Attention Mixture-of-Experts architecture, trained on massive datasets including 100+ million hours of audio-visual content. Key innovations include ARIA, a dynamic alignment system that improves streaming speech synthesis stability by better coordinating text and speech tokenizers, and support for extremely long sequences (10+ hours audio, 400 seconds video) with multilingual capabilities across 10 languages. The model achieves state-of-the-art results on 215 audio/audio-visual benchmarks and demonstrates a novel "Audio-Visual Vibe Coding" capability for programming based on multimodal instructions.

**Key points:**

- Introduces ARIA technique to dynamically align text and speech units, addressing inherent instability and unnaturalness in streaming speech synthesis caused by encoding efficiency discrepancies between tokenizers
- Demonstrates emergence of 'Audio-Visual Vibe Coding' - a novel capability enabling direct code generation from audio-visual instructions, representing a new form of multimodal programming interface
- Achieves extreme long-sequence processing capabilities with over 10 hours of audio understanding and 400 seconds of 720P video at 1 FPS using Hybrid Attention Mixture-of-Experts framework
- Scales to hundreds of billions of parameters with 256k context length while leveraging massive heterogeneous dataset of 100+ million hours of audio-visual content
- Exhibits superior audio-visual grounding with script-level structured captions featuring precise temporal synchronization and automated scene segmentation

### [Qwen3.5-Omni Technical Report](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.15804)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Qwen3.5-Omni is a large-scale multimodal model with hundreds of billions of parameters that processes text, audio, and video using a Hybrid Attention Mixture-of-Experts architecture, trained on over 100 million hours of audio-visual content. The model introduces ARIA, a dynamic alignment technique that improves speech synthesis stability by addressing mismatches between text and speech tokenizers, and achieves state-of-the-art performance on 215 audio and audio-visual benchmarks while supporting 10 languages and extended context (256k tokens, 10+ hours audio, 400 seconds video). The paper reports a novel emergent capability called "Audio-Visual Vibe Coding" where the model can generate code directly from audio-visual instructions, though no obvious limitations are discussed in the provided summary.

**Key points:**

- Introduces ARIA system for dynamic alignment of text and speech units to address instability in streaming speech synthesis caused by encoding efficiency discrepancies between text and speech tokenizers
- Achieves SOTA results across 215 audio and audio-visual benchmarks, surpassing Gemini-3.1 Pro in key audio tasks while supporting 256k context length and hundreds of billions of parameters
- Demonstrates emergence of 'Audio-Visual Vibe Coding' - a new capability allowing direct coding based on audio-visual instructions, representing novel emergent behavior in omnimodal models
- Employs Hybrid Attention Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework for both Thinker and Talker components, enabling efficient long-sequence inference for 10+ hours of audio and 400 seconds of 720P video processing
