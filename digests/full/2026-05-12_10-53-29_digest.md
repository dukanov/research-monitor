### [Evaluating the Expressive Appropriateness of Speech in Rich Contexts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09413)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

This paper introduces CEAEval, a framework for evaluating whether speech expression is contextually appropriate rather than just emotionally intense, addressing a gap in current speech evaluation methods that focus mainly on emotional intensity. The key difference is that it assesses expressive appropriateness by considering discourse-level narrative context using a new Mandarin dataset (CEAEval-D) with human annotations and a multi-component model (CEAEval-M) incorporating knowledge distillation and reinforcement learning. The main limitation is that the dataset and evaluation are currently limited to Mandarin conversational speech, which may limit broader applicability across languages and speech types.

**Key points:**

- Introduces CEAEval framework that evaluates expressive speech appropriateness based on discourse-level narrative context, addressing the limitation that existing methods only assess emotional intensity while overlooking contextual appropriateness
- Constructs CEAEval-D, the first context-rich speech dataset with real human performances in Mandarin conversational speech, featuring narrative descriptions and 15 dimensions of human annotations for expressive attributes and appropriateness
- Develops CEAEval-M model using knowledge distillation, planner-based multi-model collaboration, adaptive audio attention bias, and reinforcement learning for context-rich expressive appropriateness evaluation
- Demonstrates substantial performance improvements over existing speech evaluation and analysis systems on human-annotated test sets, addressing evaluation needs for narrative-driven applications like audiobooks and conversational agents

### [PoDAR: Power-Disentangled Audio Representation for Generative Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10084)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

**Main approach:** PoDAR introduces a framework that explicitly separates signal power from semantic content in audio representations using randomized power augmentation and latent consistency objectives, making the latent space easier for generative models to learn.

**Key difference:** Unlike recent work focused on generator expressivity or reconstruction fidelity, this targets improving "latent modelability" through explicit factor disentanglement of power and content.

**Results:** When applied to Stable Audio 1.0 VAE with F5-TTS generator, PoDAR achieves 2× faster convergence while improving speaker similarity (+0.055) and UTMOS (+0.22) scores, and enables more stable classifier-free guidance at higher scales.

**Key points:**

- PoDAR introduces explicit factor disentanglement in audio latent spaces by separating signal power from semantic content using randomized power augmentation and latent consistency objectives
- Unlike prior work focusing on generator expressivity and reconstruction fidelity, this approach specifically targets improving latent space modelability through disentanglement
- Power disentanglement achieves approximately 2x faster convergence while simultaneously improving performance metrics (speaker similarity +0.055, UTMOS +0.22 on LibriSpeech-PC)
- Isolating power into dedicated channels enables applying Classifier-Free Guidance exclusively to power-invariant content, extending stable guidance regime to higher scales
- Demonstrates that latent space structure and modelability can be as important as generator expressivity for audio generation performance

### [Kinetic-Optimal Scheduling with Moment Correction for Metric-Induced Discrete Flow Matching in Zero-Shot Text-to-Speech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09386)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces GibbsTTS, a text-to-speech system that improves metric-induced discrete flow matching (MI-DFM) by replacing heuristic schedulers with a kinetic-optimal scheduler that traverses probability paths at constant Fisher-Rao speed, and adding finite-step moment correction to reduce path-tracking errors. The key innovations are making the scheduling training-free and analytically derived rather than requiring hyperparameter search, while correcting jump probabilities in the continuous-time Markov chain solver to preserve destination distributions. The method achieves strong performance in zero-shot TTS tasks, though it builds on the relatively complex MI-DFM framework which may limit broader adoption.

**Key points:**

- Introduces kinetic-optimal scheduler that traverses probability paths at constant Fisher-Rao speed, providing a training-free numerical schedule that eliminates hyperparameter search requirements in MI-DFM
- Proposes finite-step moment correction technique that adjusts jump probability while preserving CTMC jump destination distribution to reduce path-tracking error from first-order solvers
- GibbsTTS achieves best objective naturalness in controlled comparisons and demonstrates superior speaker similarity, ranking highest on three of four test sets compared to state-of-the-art TTS systems
- Addresses two key limitations of metric-induced discrete flow matching: heuristic scheduling requiring hyperparameter tuning and finite-step path-tracking errors from continuous-time Markov chain solvers

### [SDiaReward: Modeling and Benchmarking Spoken Dialogue Rewards with Modality and Colloquialness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14889)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper introduces SDiaReward, an end-to-end reward model that evaluates spoken dialogue systems by directly processing multi-turn speech episodes and assessing both prosodic/emotional qualities (modality) and conversational naturalness (colloquialness) through pairwise preference learning. Unlike existing text-based evaluation methods, SDiaReward addresses the "modality gap" and "colloquialness gap" by operating on raw speech input and being specifically trained on preference pairs targeting these acoustic and conversational aspects. The model demonstrates superior performance compared to general-purpose audio language models on their proposed benchmark, though the evaluation is primarily conducted on their own dataset and benchmark.

**Key points:**

- Introduces SDiaReward, a novel end-to-end multi-turn reward model that addresses two critical gaps in spoken dialogue systems: the modality gap (prosody and emotion) and the colloquialness gap (written scripts vs natural speech)
- Proposes SDiaReward-Dataset with episode-level preference pairs and ESDR-Bench benchmark, enabling joint assessment of modality and colloquialness in speech dialogue evaluation
- Achieves state-of-the-art pairwise preference accuracy and significantly outperforms general-purpose audio LLMs by operating directly on full multi-turn speech episodes
- Demonstrates that the model captures relative conversational expressiveness beyond superficial synthesis cues, improving generalization across domains and recording conditions

### [ScenemaAI/scenema-audio](https://huggingface.co/ScenemaAI/scenema-audio)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

Scenema Audio is a zero-shot expressive voice cloning system built on an audio diffusion transformer extracted from LTX 2.3's 22B parameter model, trained on real conversational data to learn emotional speech patterns. Unlike standard TTS systems, it generates speech with dynamic emotional arcs, scene-aware ambient audio, and expressive acting capabilities controlled through XML prompts with detailed voice descriptions and action tags, requiring only 10-20 seconds of reference audio for voice cloning. The system requires substantial computational resources (~38GB of models) and uses a complex pipeline involving text encoding, latent diffusion, and audio VAE decoding.

**Key points:**

- Zero-shot expressive voice cloning using only 10-20 seconds of reference audio with no fine-tuning required, transferring voice identity onto any emotional performance even if that voice was never recorded in that emotional state
- Audio diffusion transformer extracted from LTX 2.3's 22B parameter audiovisual model that learned from real scene audio, enabling generation of speech with intention, pacing, breath control, and emotional arcs that shift within single generations
- Scene-aware audio generation capability that produces environmental ambient audio (rain, thunder, crowds) alongside speech based on scene descriptions in the prompt
- Novel XML prompt format with action tags for real-time emotional direction, allowing emotional state shifts and performance directions within a single audio generation
- Architecture combines Gemma 3 12B text encoding with 8-step distilled latent diffusion and audio VAE decoding, with MelBandRoFormer vocal separation for SFX control
