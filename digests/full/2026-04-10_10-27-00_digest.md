### [CapTalk: Unified Voice Design for Single-Utterance and Dialogue Speech Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08363)

**Relevance:** 75.0%

CapTalk introduces a unified framework for generating speech from natural language voice descriptions, extending beyond single-utterance synthesis to handle multi-turn dialogue contexts. The key innovation is using speaker-level captions for dialogue modeling combined with a Chain-of-Thought control sequence for turn-level expression planning, plus a hierarchical variational conditioning module that balances consistent speaker timbre with context-adaptive expression. While the approach shows strong results on benchmarks, the framework's complexity suggests potential challenges in training stability and computational requirements, though specific limitations aren't detailed in the abstract.

**Key points:**

- CapTalk introduces the first unified framework for both single-utterance and dialogue voice design, extending beyond existing methods that only handle single utterances to enable conversational speech synthesis with turn-level expressive control
- The framework addresses a key technical challenge by using a hierarchical variational conditioning module to balance stable timbre preservation with context-adaptive expression, allowing timbre reuse while adapting expression to surrounding dialogue context
- Novel CoT (Chain of Thought) control sequence is introduced for dialogue settings to explicitly plan turn-level dynamic attributes, enabling better contextual appropriateness in multi-turn conversations
- The method uses different caption strategies: utterance-level captions for single-utterance voice design and speaker-level captions for dialogue speaker modeling, providing more flexible control mechanisms
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on single-utterance benchmarks while demonstrating superior expression controllability and contextual appropriateness in multi-turn dialogue compared to existing approaches

### [Lexical Tone is Hard to Quantize: Probing Discrete Speech Units in Mandarin and Yor\`ub\'a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07467)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

This paper investigates how well discrete speech units (DSUs) from self-supervised learning models preserve lexical tone information in Mandarin and Yorùbá, finding that while the original SSL representations encode tone, standard quantization methods like K-means prioritize phonetic structure and lose suprasegmental information. The authors demonstrate this limitation across multiple quantization approaches and propose a two-stage clustering solution that first encodes phonetic information, then applies clustering to residual representations to better capture tone. The work highlights a fundamental limitation of current DSU quantization strategies for tone languages and prosodic features more broadly.

**Key points:**

- SSL latent representations do encode lexical tone information, but quantization methods (including K-means) tend to prioritize phonetic structure and lose suprasegmental features like tone
- Current discrete speech unit (DSU) quantization strategies have fundamental limitations for suprasegmental features, which likely extends beyond tone to other prosodic elements
- The authors propose a novel two-stage K-means approach: first clustering for phonetic information, then clustering the residual representation to better capture lexical tone
- Testing on Mandarin and Yoruba demonstrates that DSUs encode segmental structure more reliably than suprasegmental information, revealing a systematic bias in current quantization methods
- The findings suggest a need for new tone-aware and prosody-aware techniques in speech representation learning to address these quantization limitations
