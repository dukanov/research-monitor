### [AffectCodec: Emotion-Preserving Neural Speech Codec with Block-Diagonal Residual FSQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23373)

**Relevance:** 80.0%

AffectCodec introduces a neural speech codec that explicitly preserves emotional information during compression by using Block-Diagonal Residual Finite Scalar Quantization (BD-RFSQ), which structurally separates emotion and acoustic subspaces to prevent emotion-relevant cues from being lost during quantization. Unlike existing codecs that optimize primarily for acoustic reconstruction fidelity and implicitly allocate bits through loss-driven mechanisms, this approach provides explicit structural guarantees for emotion preservation through block-diagonal input/output projections. The method shows improved emotion preservation at low bitrates while maintaining competitive acoustic quality, though the approach is specifically designed for emotional speech rather than demonstrating broader attribute preservation capabilities.

**Key points:**

- AffectCodec introduces Block-Diagonal Residual Finite Scalar Quantization (BD-RFSQ) that explicitly separates emotion and acoustic subspaces through structural constraints, transforming bit allocation from implicit loss-driven to explicit structurally-guaranteed allocation
- The paper identifies two key mechanisms causing emotion degradation in neural speech codecs: reconstruction-driven bit allocation under limited bitrate and cross-stream leakage where acoustic gradients overwrite emotion-reserved dimensions
- BD-RFSQ maintains a flat token interface for downstream speech language models while providing structural protection for emotion-relevant information through block-diagonal input/output projections
- Experiments show substantial improvements in emotion preservation especially at low bitrates while maintaining competitive acoustic quality and intelligibility across multiple emotional speech benchmarks
- The work suggests structurally protected quantization as a general principle for attribute-aware neural speech compression beyond just emotion preservation

### [UniSRM: A Unified Speech Reward Model for Reasoning-Based Fine-grained Assessment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23261)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

UniSRM proposes a unified speech reward model that evaluates speech generation across multiple dimensions (from utterance-level quality to context-level coherence) using a two-stage reasoning-based assessment pipeline, moving beyond expensive human judgments like MOS. Unlike existing AudioLLM-based judges that target narrow scenarios, UniSRM aims to provide comprehensive, interpretable evaluation across diverse speech generation tasks through new datasets (UniSRM-Data/Bench) and "Reasoning-Consistent Rewards" for improved reliability. The work addresses the scalability challenge of speech evaluation, though the approach's computational requirements and generalization across different speech synthesis architectures are not discussed in the abstract.

**Key points:**

- UniSRM introduces a unified speech reward model that provides multi-dimensional, interpretable reward signals with reasoning-based fine-grained assessment, addressing limitations of expensive and subjective human evaluation methods like MOS
- Unlike existing AudioLLM-based judge models that target narrow scenarios, UniSRM covers diverse speech evaluation tasks from utterance-level quality to context-level coherence with broader task coverage
- The model employs a novel two-stage pipeline with Reasoning-Consistent Rewards mechanism to improve the reliability of the reasoning process in speech evaluation
- UniSRM demonstrates more reliable and human-aligned judgments across a broad range of speech evaluation tasks, offering a practical foundation for scalable unified speech quality assessment

### [StepAudio 2.5 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23463)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

StepAudio 2.5 presents a unified audio-language foundation model that achieves state-of-the-art performance across ASR, TTS, and real-time dialogue by using a shared multimodal representation space with task-specific "operational regimes" rather than separate architectures. The key innovation is using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) as the primary post-training mechanism to shape the shared backbone into distinct operational modes for each speech task, combined with specialized decoding strategies. The approach demonstrates that a single foundation model can match specialized systems across all three capabilities, though the technical details of the unified representation learning and RLHF implementation would need further examination to assess practical limitations.

**Key points:**

- Introduces a paradigm shift from treating ASR, TTS, and realtime interaction as architecturally distinct tasks to viewing them as different operational regimes within a shared multimodal representational space
- Advances post-training from standard supervised learning to task-tailored RLHF as the primary mechanism for defining complex optimization targets across speech tasks
- Demonstrates that a single unified audio-language foundation model can match or exceed specialized systems across ASR, TTS, and realtime interaction through operational regime differentiation
- Achieves task specialization through data construction, optimization targets, and decoding constraints rather than architectural differences once multimodal representation is established
- Implements novel technical approaches: verifiable multi-token decoding for ASR efficiency, preference-based RLHF for controllable TTS synthesis, and generative reward modeling for low-latency realtime dialogue

### [StepAudio 2.5 Technical Report](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.23463)

**Relevance:** 70.0%

StepAudio 2.5 is a unified audio-language foundation model that uses a single shared backbone to handle automatic speech recognition (ASR), text-to-speech synthesis (TTS), and real-time spoken interaction by employing different operational regimes (data construction, optimization targets, and decoding constraints) rather than separate architectures for each task. The key innovation is using task-tailored Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) as the primary mechanism for specializing the shared model to each task during post-training, rather than relying on standard supervised learning. The authors claim this approach allows their unified model to match or exceed the performance of specialized systems across all three speech capabilities, which existing unified models have struggled to achieve.

**Key points:**

- StepAudio 2.5 introduces a novel unified approach treating ASR, TTS, and real-time interaction as different operational regimes of a shared backbone rather than architecturally distinct tasks
- Advances post-training paradigm from standard supervised learning to task-tailored RLHF as the primary mechanism for defining complex optimization targets across speech tasks
- Achieves state-of-the-art results across all three speech capabilities (ASR, TTS, realtime dialogue) using a single foundation model, demonstrating that unified models can match specialized systems
- Implements task-specific innovations: verifiable multi-token decoding for ASR efficiency, preference-based RLHF for controllable TTS synthesis, and generative reward modeling for low-latency dialogue
- Operates on the key insight that once text and audio share multimodal representational space, task specialization becomes a matter of data construction, optimization targets, and decoding constraints rather than architectural differences
